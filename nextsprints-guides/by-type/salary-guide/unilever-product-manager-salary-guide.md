## Unilever Product Manager Salary Overview

In 2026, Unilever's Product Manager salaries remain competitive within the FMCG industry, reflecting the company's commitment to attracting and retaining top talent. The general salary range for PMs at Unilever in major European hubs spans roughly €60,000 to €180,000 annually, depending on experience, location, and performance. Pay in other markets differs widely: a UK posting for a digital commerce product manager listed £50,000 - £70,000, and Levels.fyi shows a median total compensation of about ₹33 lakh for Unilever PMs in India. These figures are 2026 estimates based on Levels.fyi and public job postings. Note that Unilever completed the demerger of its ice cream business (The Magnum Ice Cream Company) in December 2025, so ice cream PM roles now sit at a separate company.

Key factors influencing PM salaries at Unilever include:

1. Years of experience in product management
2. Educational background and relevant certifications
3. Performance and impact on product success
4. Geographic location of the role
5. Specific product category and its strategic importance

Compared to industry standards, Unilever's PM compensation package is generally on par with other major FMCG companies. However, Unilever often differentiates itself through its strong focus on sustainability and purpose-driven work, which can be attractive to candidates who prioritize these values.

2. This multi-faceted approach ensures that Product Managers are rewarded for both short-term performance and long-term value creation, while also incentivizing sustainable product development practices.

## How Location Affects Unilever PM Salaries

Geographic location plays a significant role in determining Product Manager salaries at Unilever. In 2026, the company maintains a global presence with varying compensation levels to reflect local market conditions and cost of living.

Key location-based salary trends:

1. Major European hubs (London, Rotterdam, Hamburg): Salaries tend to be at the higher end of the ranges Additional allowances for high cost-of-living areas
2. Emerging markets (India, Brazil, Indonesia): Salaries are generally lower but highly competitive within local markets Often include additional benefits like housing or transportation allowances
3. North America (US, Canada): Salaries are typically higher than European counterparts More emphasis on equity compensation
4. Remote work: Unilever has embraced a flexible working model Base salaries for remote workers are generally adjusted based on their location
5. International assignments: Typically include a premium or allowances on top of base salary Additional allowances for relocation, housing, and education

Unilever's global mobility program allows PMs to gain international experience, which can significantly impact career growth and compensation.
