## Introduction

Jupiter, the money app built by Amica Financial Technologies, hires product managers for its savings, payments, credit card, lending and insurance products. As a growth-stage fintech that has set a goal of profitability within about two years, it pays competitively for Bengaluru product talent but keeps cash bands tighter than the largest consumer internet companies. This guide gives 2026 estimates for Jupiter PM pay by level in INR, explains how pay is structured, and offers tips for negotiating an offer.

## Understanding Jupiter's PM Compensation Structure

**Fixed salary.** The largest part of pay at every level. Jupiter, like most Indian startups, focuses on fixed pay rather than large bonuses.

**Variable pay.** Where offered, usually 5–10% of fixed pay, tied to company and individual performance. Some offers have no variable component.

**ESOPs.** Granted at joining and sometimes as refreshers, typically vesting over four years with a one-year cliff. With a reported valuation of around $600 million in 2025, ESOPs can be meaningful, but their value depends on future fundraising, liquidity events or an exit. Candidates should ask about the strike price, vesting schedule and any past buyback programmes.

**Joining bonus.** Sometimes offered to bridge a bonus the candidate gives up at their current employer.

**Benefits.** Health insurance for the employee and family, flexible leave, learning budgets and, at many Bengaluru fintechs, hybrid work policies.

Example package for a Product Manager with four years of experience:

- Fixed: ₹28 L
- Variable: ₹2 L (target)
- ESOPs: ₹20 L notional value over four years (about ₹5 L a year)
- Total yearly value: around ₹35 L including notional ESOP value

## Jupiter Product Manager Salaries by Level

### Associate PM / PM 1

Usually for candidates with one to three years of experience, often from engineering, analytics or a startup. Pay ranges from about ₹14 L to ₹22 L total cash. Strong MBA graduates from top schools may be offered pay at the upper end. Associate PMs typically own a feature within a larger product, such as Jewels redemption or a Pots flow.

### Product Manager

For three to five years of experience. Total cash of about ₹22 L to ₹38 L. PMs own a full product or journey, such as UPI payments or card activation, and work directly with design, engineering and data.

### Senior PM

For five to eight years of experience, matching recent job posts for Senior PMs in Core Experience, Lending and Credit Cards. Total cash of about ₹38 L to ₹60 L, plus a larger ESOP grant. Senior PMs own a business line, set metrics, and work with partner banks and risk teams.

### Lead / Principal PM

For eight to twelve years. About ₹55 L to ₹80 L total cash. They lead several PMs or a whole area like credit, and contribute to company strategy.

### Head / Director of Product

For senior leaders with twelve or more years of experience. About ₹80 L to ₹1.3 Cr total cash, with significant ESOPs. They report to founders and shape the product portfolio.

## Career Progression and Salary Growth

| Move | Typical time | Typical pay jump |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Associate PM → PM | 1.5–2.5 years | 25–40% |
| PM → Senior PM | 2–3 years | 30–45% |
| Senior PM → Lead PM | 2–4 years | 25–35% |
| Lead PM → Head of Product | 3+ years | 30–50% |

Annual increments for good performance are usually 8–15%, with larger jumps at promotion. Because the company is smaller than big tech, strong PMs can take on more scope quickly, for example moving from a feature to a full product line, which speeds up promotion.

Experience at Jupiter is valued in the market. PMs with credit card, lending or account aggregator experience are in demand at other fintechs, banks' digital units and NBFCs.

## Negotiating Your Jupiter PM Offer

**1. Know the bands.** Use AmbitionBox, Glassdoor and conversations with PMs at similar fintechs (Fi Money, Slice, CRED, OneCard) to understand market pay.

**2. Negotiate fixed pay first.** In a startup, fixed pay is the most certain part of compensation. ESOPs are valuable but uncertain.

**3. Ask detailed ESOP questions.**

- How many options, at what strike price?
- What was the valuation at the last round?
- Has the company run buybacks before?
- What happens to vested options if you leave?

**4. Highlight fintech experience.** Experience with UPI, cards, lending risk or compliance can justify a higher band.

**5. Consider a joining bonus.** If you are giving up a pending bonus or unvested stock, ask for a one-time joining bonus.

**6. Be realistic about timing.** The company is focused on profitability, so expect careful cash offers but some flexibility in ESOPs.

## Conclusion

Jupiter offers competitive PM pay for a growth-stage fintech, with total cash of roughly ₹14 L–₹22 L for associate PMs, ₹22 L–₹38 L for PMs and ₹38 L–₹60 L for Senior PMs in 2026, plus ESOPs. The real draw is scope: PMs own important money journeys in one of India's best-designed fintech apps. Candidates should weigh fixed pay, ESOP upside and learning opportunity together.

## FAQs

### Does Jupiter pay more than other Indian fintechs?

Jupiter's PM pay is around the middle to upper-middle of Indian fintech startups. AmbitionBox suggests it is above the national PM average. Larger, later-stage companies such as CRED or PhonePe may pay more in cash at senior levels.

### Are ESOPs at Jupiter worth much?

They can be, but their value depends on future funding rounds, buybacks or an exit. The 2025 round was reportedly at a flat valuation, so treat ESOP value as long-term upside rather than guaranteed pay.

### What do Jupiter PM interns earn?

PM intern stipends at Bengaluru fintechs like Jupiter are typically ₹60,000 to ₹1,20,000 a month, depending on the school and the length of the internship.

### Do credit and lending PMs earn more than other PMs at Jupiter?

Bands are usually the same by level, but PMs with credit, risk or lending experience are scarce, so they may be offered pay at the top of a band.
