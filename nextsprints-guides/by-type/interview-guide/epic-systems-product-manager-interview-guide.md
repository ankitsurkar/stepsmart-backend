## Introduction

Epic Systems is the largest electronic health record (EHR) company in the US, headquartered on a large campus in Verona, Wisconsin, near Madison. Its software runs a large share of US hospitals and academic medical centres, and its products include Hyperspace (the clinical workspace), MyChart (the patient portal and app used by well over a hundred million patients), Cosmos (a de-identified dataset covering about 320 million patients), and a fast-growing set of AI features under the names Art, Emmie and Penny.

Epic is unusual for PM candidates: it does not use the "product manager" title in the way tech companies do. Product work is spread across several roles that hire large numbers of new graduates each year:

- **Software Developers** design and build features end to end, including writing the design specification, talking to customers and deciding how the feature works.
- **Quality Managers (QMs)** test software against real clinical workflows and act as the voice of the user before release.
- **Project Managers** (implementation) lead customers through installing and optimising Epic, and feed customer needs back into development.
- **Technical Solutions Engineers**, **UX designers** and **Customer Success** staff complete the picture.

Students who want "product" experience in healthcare often target these roles. This guide explains how product work is done at Epic, how the hiring process works and how to prepare.

## PM Role

> Product Work at Epic
> At Epic, "product management" is distributed. Software developers own the design of the features they build, quality managers own whether those features work in real workflows, and project managers own customers' success with the software. Senior developers and team leads set direction for an application area, such as MyChart, Cosmos or inpatient nursing.

How product decisions happen:

- **Customer input**: Ideas come from customer requests, user group meetings (UGM in August, XGM in spring), advisory councils and site visits.
- **Developer-led design**: A developer writes a design document describing the problem, workflow and solution, reviews it with team leads and sometimes customers, then builds it.
- **Quality management**: QMs test features with realistic clinical scenarios and catch workflow gaps.
- **Release**: Epic now ships releases roughly every three months, so features reach customers faster than under the old 18-month cycle.
- **Adoption**: Implementation and technical services teams help customers turn features on; Epic tracks this through its "Gold Stars" programme.

Responsibilities by role:

| Role | Product-related responsibilities |
| --- | --- |
| Software Developer | Feature design, coding, customer conversations, design reviews, supporting go-lives |
| Quality Manager | Test planning, workflow testing, usability feedback, release readiness |
| Project Manager | Implementation planning, customer workflow decisions, training, optimisation, travel to customer sites |
| Technical Solutions Engineer | Integrations, system performance, customer technical support |

| Work authorisation | Epic sponsors some roles; check postings | Varies | Varies |

Traits Epic looks for:

- Strong academic record and quantitative reasoning.
- Curiosity and willingness to learn healthcare.
- Comfort with relocating to the Madison area and working on campus.
- Customer focus; Epic's culture emphasises doing what's right for patients and customers.

> Common Pitfalls
> Candidates sometimes talk about "managing products" in abstract tech terms. Epic interviewers prefer concrete examples of solving problems, learning quickly and caring about users.

> Expert Advice
> Learn the basics of healthcare workflows: what nurses do at shift change, how orders flow, what a patient portal does. Even simple familiarity makes your answers stand out.

## Interview Process Breakdown

    A[Online application] --> B[Role introduction or recruiter screen]

| Stage | Format | Duration | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Online application | Epic careers site | n/a | Resume, transcript, GPA, test scores sometimes requested |
| Role introduction / screen | Phone or video | 20–30 min | Interest in role, relocation, logistics |
| Skills assessment | Online, timed | 2–3 hours | Logic, math, reading comprehension, programming-style puzzles |
| Personality or situational questionnaire | Online | 30–45 min | Work style, stress handling (reported for some roles) |
| Interview day | Virtual or on campus in Verona | Half day | Behavioural interviews, problem solving, role-specific exercises |
| Offer | Recruiter call | n/a | Salary, start date, relocation |

Detailed breakdown:

- Skills assessment:

Candidates describe logic puzzles, math word problems and a section where you learn a made-up programming language and answer questions using its rules. No prior coding is required for non-developer roles. Many candidates call it hard and say practice on SAT or ACT style math and logic helps.

- Developer track:

Additional programming questions or exercises; interviewers look for clear problem solving more than knowledge of a specific language.

- Quality Manager track:

Interviews test attention to detail, logical thinking and how you'd test a workflow. Some candidates mention questions on handling stressful situations and time management.

- Project Manager track:

Scenario questions about leading meetings, handling difficult customers, organising competing tasks and communicating clearly.

- Interview day:

Several conversations with Epic staff, often including a tour of the campus when on site. Expect questions such as "Tell me about a time you solved a hard problem" and "How would you explain a technical concept to a non-technical person?"

Timeline expectations: Three to eight weeks from application to offer, with the skills assessment usually within a week or two of applying.

## How to Prepare

1. **Practise the skills assessment**: Logic puzzles, math word problems and pattern recognition. Time yourself.
2. **Learn Epic's products**: MyChart, Hyperspace, Haiku and Rover mobile apps, Cosmos, and AI assistants Art, Emmie and Penny.
3. **Understand healthcare basics**: EHRs, patient portals, clinical workflows, billing and interoperability.
4. **Prepare behavioural stories**: Problem solving, learning quickly, teamwork, handling pressure, helping users.
5. **Practise product questions**: Even though Epic doesn't run formal PM case interviews, product sense helps in design discussions and project manager scenarios. Practise improving MyChart features or designing tools for nurses.
6. **Think about relocation**: Be ready to explain why you want to live and work in the Madison area.

## FAQs

### Does Epic Systems hire product managers?

Epic rarely uses the product manager title. Product responsibilities are shared by software developers (who design features), quality managers (who ensure features work for users) and project managers (who help customers implement and use the software). Students targeting PM careers often treat these roles as product-adjacent entry points.

### How hard is the Epic skills assessment?

Candidates describe it as challenging and long, up to around three hours, with logic, math and a made-up programming language. It is heavily weighted in hiring decisions. Practice with timed logic and math problems is the best preparation.

### Do I need a computer science degree to work at Epic?

No. Software developer roles prefer technical backgrounds but accept other majors with strong problem-solving ability. Quality manager and project manager roles welcome any major.

### Can I work remotely at Epic?

Epic has a strong on-campus culture in Verona. Most roles require relocating to the Madison area, and project managers travel to customer sites. Remote options are limited.

### How do Epic roles prepare you for a product career?

Developers own feature design and customer conversations, which builds product sense. Quality managers learn user workflows deeply, and project managers gain customer and stakeholder management skills. Many people later move into product roles at health tech companies.
