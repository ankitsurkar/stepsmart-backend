## Executive Summary

Wipro Intelligence is Wipro's unified suite of AI-powered platforms, solutions and offerings, built to help enterprises build and transform AI-first capabilities. It brings together WEGA, Wipro's agent-native AI delivery platform; WINGS, AI agents for IT run operations; the Lab45 AI platform for GenAI apps; and industry solutions, building on the lineage of Wipro HOLMES and the Wipro ai360 investment programme. Three factors define it: 1) a services-as-software model, where outcomes are delivered by agents supervised by Wipro experts; 2) multi-model, multi-cloud flexibility through partnerships with Google Cloud, NVIDIA, Microsoft and AWS; and 3) deep operations experience from Wipro's managed services business.

Its unique value proposition is "AI outcomes delivered and governed by a partner that already runs your operations". Its challenges are brand recognition against larger rivals, proving reliable outcomes at scale and shifting commercial models from people to platforms.

This teardown analyses the product, user journeys, design, features, business model and competitors.

## Introduction

Enterprises in 2026 are moving from GenAI experiments to agents that do real work: resolving IT tickets, processing invoices, triaging claims and testing software. Most lack the skills to build, run and govern hundreds of agents safely. IT services firms see an opportunity to package these capabilities as platforms plus services.

Wipro's AI journey has several stages. Wipro HOLMES, its earlier AI and automation platform, powered virtual agents and cognitive process automation. In July 2023, Wipro launched ai360 with a US$1 billion, three-year investment commitment. In June 2024, Lab45 made its AI platform available to all employees and clients. In 2025, Wipro launched agentic AI services with NVIDIA, and the Wipro Intelligence brand unified its AI platforms. In 2026, HFS Research named Wipro a leader in agentic services.

This analysis uses public information, analyst coverage and industry knowledge; figures are estimates.

## Product Overview

Core value proposition: enable enterprises to deploy AI agents and GenAI applications that deliver measurable outcomes, with governance, security and Wipro's operational expertise.

Target users and buyers:

1. **Buyers:** CIOs, COOs and heads of shared services in large enterprises
2. **Business users:** Process owners in IT, finance, HR and customer service
3. **Builders:** Wipro delivery teams and client developers
4. **End users:** Employees and customers who interact with agents

Components:

| Component | Role |
| --- | --- |
| WEGA | Platform for building, deploying and running AI agents across delivery and transformation work |
| WINGS | Agents for IT service delivery and operations, shifting support from reactive to proactive |
| Lab45 AI platform | Multi-model GenAI platform with no-code apps and APIs |
| Industry solutions | Banking, capital markets, regulatory, insurance and other domain offerings |
| HOLMES lineage | Earlier automation and virtual agent capabilities |
| ai360 | Investment and talent programme behind the suite |

## User Journey Deep-Dive

Client journey:

1. **Discovery:** Wipro and the client identify processes suited to agents (high volume, rule-heavy, measurable).
2. **Design and pilot:** Agents are configured on WEGA or WINGS, integrated with client systems, tested on historical data.
3. **Deploy:** Agents go live with human oversight; outcomes are measured.
4. **Scale:** More processes added; autonomy increased as performance proves out.
5. **Govern and improve:** Continuous monitoring, retraining and audits.

Pain points:

- Integration with legacy systems and identity management
- Defining outcomes and baselines for pricing
- Change management for client staff
- Visibility of agent performance across many processes

Employee journey (for WINGS): an employee raises a request in Teams, an agent understands it, takes action (for example, resets access or installs software) and confirms. Moments of truth: first-contact resolution and smooth handoff to humans when agents cannot help.

## UX & Design Analysis

Design priorities:

- **Conversational interfaces** in tools employees already use (Teams, portals)
- **Builder experiences** for configuring agents and apps with low code
- **Governance dashboards** for leaders to see outcomes, costs and risks
- **Transparency:** explanations and audit logs for agent actions

Strengths:

- Integration into familiar workplace tools
- Multi-model flexibility through Lab45

Areas to improve:

- A single, unified console across WEGA, WINGS and Lab45
- Clearer outcome reporting for client executives
- Self-serve onboarding for smaller clients

| UX dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| End-user conversational experience | Good |
| Builder experience | Moderate, improving |
| Governance and observability | Moderate |
| Executive reporting | Developing |

## Business Model Analysis

Revenue streams:

1. **Outcome-priced services:** per ticket resolved, per invoice processed, per test cycle
2. **Managed services enhanced by agents:** traditional contracts with productivity commitments
3. **Platform and consulting fees:** implementation, integration and advisory
4. **Licences and subscriptions:** for platform components used by clients directly

Economics:

- Agents reduce delivery cost, improving margins if pricing is outcome-based
- Under time-and-materials pricing, AI productivity can reduce revenue
- Upfront investment in platforms is high; payback depends on reuse across clients

Strategic tension: Wipro must shift sales incentives, contracts and revenue recognition to benefit from its platforms, while protecting near-term revenue.

## Competitive Analysis

| Accenture | AI platforms and agent services | Scale, consulting brand | Premium pricing |

Differentiation:

- Combination of platforms and large-scale operations delivery
- Analyst recognition in agentic services
- Multi-cloud and multi-model approach

Threats:

- Software vendors embedding agents directly into their products
- Price pressure from rivals and startups
- Execution risk in scaling outcome-based contracts

## FAQs

### What makes Wipro Intelligence unique in the market?

It combines agent platforms (WEGA, WINGS, Lab45) with Wipro's experience running clients' operations, so it can sell outcomes rather than tools. Analyst recognition in agentic services supports this positioning.

### How does Wipro Intelligence make money?

Through outcome-priced services, agent-enhanced managed services, consulting and implementation fees, and platform subscriptions. The long-term aim is a larger share of outcome-based revenue.

### What are Wipro Intelligence's standout features?

Agent orchestration on WEGA, IT operations agents in WINGS, multi-model GenAI apps on the Lab45 AI platform, and industry solutions in financial services.

### How has Wipro Intelligence evolved since launch?

It builds on Wipro HOLMES automation, the 2023 ai360 investment, the 2024 Lab45 AI platform launch and 2025 agentic AI services, unified under the Wipro Intelligence brand and recognised by analysts in 2026.
