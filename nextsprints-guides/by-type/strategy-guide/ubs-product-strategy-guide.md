## Executive Summary

In 2026 UBS is completing the largest bank integration in Swiss history. Its product strategy is shaped by three shifts:

1. From integration to simplification: UBS finished migrating all former Credit Suisse clients onto its own platforms in March 2026, with retention that management said exceeded expectations. The remaining work, decommissioning Credit Suisse's legacy IT, is expected to be largely completed by the end of 2026, freeing capacity for new product investment.
2. From wealth manager to wealth platform: Global Wealth Management, the world's largest wealth franchise, is investing in advisor productivity, digital mandates such as UBS My Way, and client intelligence tools, with a particular focus on improving US profitability.
3. From pilots to AI at scale: UBS has rolled out its in-house generative AI assistant Red and Microsoft 365 Copilot across the firm and reported hundreds of live AI use cases by the end of 2025.

UBS's challenge is not scale but focus: it must turn a merged, complex technology estate into simpler, faster platforms, while facing intense political debate in Switzerland about its size and capital requirements.

## Introduction

UBS has four main business divisions: Global Wealth Management, Personal & Corporate Banking in Switzerland, Asset Management and the Investment Bank. Product strategy is set within each division but shares common platforms built by Group Technology, including large technology centres in India (Pune, Hyderabad, Mumbai), Poland, Switzerland and the US.

Key strategic questions for UBS:

1. How can it convert the cost savings from the Credit Suisse integration into better digital experiences rather than just margins?
2. How should it compete for the next generation of Swiss clients against neobanks such as Neon, Yuh and Revolut?
3. How can it lift US wealth margins closer to peers like Morgan Stanley?
4. How should it deploy generative AI safely in one of the world's most regulated businesses?

For candidates, understanding these questions is essential. UBS interviewers expect product owners to see how their backlog connects to these bigger goals.

Strengths:

- Unmatched wealth franchise and Chief Investment Office content.
- Dominant Swiss market position after absorbing Credit Suisse.

Weaknesses:

- Complex, partly duplicated technology estate during decommissioning.
- Consumer digital experiences that trail neobanks on simplicity.
- US wealth margins below peers.

## Short-Term: The Next 12 Months

Priorities through late 2027:

1. **Finish decommissioning**: Switch off remaining Credit Suisse applications and data centres, archive data, and redeploy teams. Product owners will spend much of their time on cutover plans, data quality and client communication.
2. **Retain migrated clients**: Improve the UBS app for former Credit Suisse and CSX users, with clearer fees, rebuilt "missing" features and better onboarding to UBS Access.
3. **Embed AI into workflows**: Move Red from a chat tool to embedded features in advisor and operations tools, such as automated meeting preparation and document summarisation, with grounded answers and citations.
4. **Advisor productivity in the US**: Automate onboarding paperwork and expand client-intelligence alerts that brief advisors before meetings.
5. **key4 growth**: Push salary-account switching and simple investing to make key4 a primary bank for young Swiss clients.

| Initiative | Expected outcome | Key metric |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legacy decommissioning | Lower run costs, fewer incidents | Systems retired, cost saved |
| Post-migration app improvements | Higher engagement among ex-CS clients | Engagement parity, closures |
| AI workflow embedding | Productivity gains | Validated hours saved |
| US advisor automation | Higher revenue per advisor | Admin hours per advisor |
| key4 primary banking | More young primary clients | Salary switch rate |

> Interview Angle
> If asked "What would you prioritise at UBS next year?", tie your answer to finishing integration well. A strong answer shows how product work protects client retention while costs come down.

## Mid-Term: 1-5 Year Outlook

1. **Platform consolidation**: Fewer, shared platforms for onboarding, payments, portfolio management and client data across regions, built on cloud infrastructure. Expect more product roles focused on reusable platforms rather than country-specific solutions.
2. **Digital wealth for the next generation**: As wealth transfers to younger heirs, UBS will need app experiences that match neobrokers for transparency and speed, while keeping advisor relationships at the core. Hybrid models combining digital tools and human advice are likely.
3. **Pension and investing in Switzerland**: Pillar 3a investing, ETF savings plans and retirement planning tools are a battleground with Yuh, VIAC, finpension and cantonal banks.
4. **AI agents with guardrails**: From assistants that answer questions to agents that complete tasks, such as preparing a client proposal or resolving a payment exception, with human approval.
5. **Asia growth**: Wealth creation in Asia remains a growth engine; digital tools for relationship managers in Singapore and Hong Kong will matter.

Risks in this horizon include Swiss regulatory changes to capital requirements that could limit investment budgets, execution risk on platform consolidation, and competition for technology talent.

## Long-Term: 5-10 Year Projection

Possible directions by the mid-2030s:

- **Wealth as a platform**: UBS may offer its investment content, portfolio construction and custody to other banks and independent advisors, similar to how some firms became infrastructure providers.
- **Personalised advice at scale**: AI-driven planning that tailors advice to each client's goals, tax situation and values, with advisors focusing on complex decisions and relationships.
- **Tokenised assets**: UBS has experimented with tokenised funds and digital assets; if markets mature, tokenisation could change how private market investments are distributed to wealthy clients.
- **A simpler Swiss bank**: One digital front door for retail clients, potentially merging key4 and the standard UBS app experiences.

Scenarios:

| Scenario | Description | Implication for product teams |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base case | Integration complete, steady wealth growth | Focus on platform quality and AI productivity |
| Upside | Strong US turnaround and Asia growth | More investment in client-facing innovation |
| Downside | Tough capital rules, market downturn | Cost focus, fewer new initiatives, more automation |

## Strategic Recommendations

1. **Reinvest integration savings into client experience**: Visible improvements for former Credit Suisse clients reduce churn and strengthen the Swiss franchise's reputation.
2. **Make AI measurable**: Track validated time saved and quality, not just licences, and scale only use cases with proven value.
3. **Use key4 as a feeder, not a discount channel**: Build a clear path from a first account to investing, mortgages and wealth services, with pricing fences to protect premium packages.
4. **Fix US advisor economics through tooling**: Automation and banking products (lending, deposits) are the fastest margin levers.
5. **Build reusable platforms**: Every product investment should reduce duplication across regions.

> For Candidates
> In strategy interviews, show awareness of UBS's constraints: political scrutiny, capital rules and integration fatigue. Recommendations that ignore them will sound naive.

## Key Takeaways

- 2026 marks the end of the Credit Suisse migration and the start of a simplification phase.
- Wealth management remains the core, with advisor productivity and US margins as key focus areas.
- AI is moving from rollout to embedded workflows; measuring real value is the next challenge.
- key4 is UBS's bet on younger Swiss clients and must avoid cannibalising premium offerings.
- Product owners who can combine delivery discipline, regulatory awareness and client focus are in demand.

Bottom line: UBS's next five years depend on turning a giant integration into a simpler, more digital bank, without losing the trust that makes it the world's leading wealth manager.
