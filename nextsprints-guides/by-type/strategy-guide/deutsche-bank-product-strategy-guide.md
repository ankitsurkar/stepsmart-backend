## Executive Summary

In 2026, Deutsche Bank is executing the next phase of its transformation. After years of restructuring, the bank presented "Scaling the Global Hausbank" at its November 2025 Investor Deep Dive, targeting a return on tangible equity above 13% by 2028 and a higher payout ratio from 2026. Its product strategy now rests on three shifts:

1. From restructuring to growth: Scaling capital-light businesses such as payments, cash management, FX and wealth, rather than relying on balance-sheet-heavy lending.
2. From batch to real time: Rebuilding corporate and retail payments around instant rails, APIs and, increasingly, tokenised money.
3. From two retail banks to one platform: Running Deutsche Bank and Postbank on one IT platform since the 2023 Unity migration, while closing branches and moving Postbank to a mobile-first model.

Deutsche Bank's strategic direction is clear: be the leading bank in Germany and the gateway to Europe for clients worldwide, with digital products that make relationship banking scalable. Execution risk remains real, as the troubled Postbank migration showed, and global banks like J.P. Morgan and Citi set a high bar in corporate digital banking.

## Introduction

Deutsche Bank's product decisions over the past two years show a bank modernising its plumbing while defending its core franchises. In payments, it launched dbX for correspondent banking in October 2024, partnered with Serrala on a real-time payments API for treasuries, and rolled out Wero to Deutsche Bank and Postbank customers in December 2025. In retail, it upgraded online banking and the Deutsche Bank app in August 2025 and replaced photoTAN with BestSign as the default authentication for new devices. In digital assets, it has explored stablecoins and tokenised deposits and prepared a crypto custody service with Taurus.

These moves respond to an industry in transition. Instant payments became mandatory in the euro area, ISO 20022 changed payment data, European policymakers pushed for payment sovereignty, and stablecoins and tokenised assets moved from experiments to regulated products under MiCA. In this context, Deutsche Bank faces several strategic questions:

1. How can a global corporate bank make mid-sized clients profitable through digital self-service?
2. How should it defend euro clearing and correspondent banking as money becomes programmable?
3. What role should Postbank play in a mobile-first retail strategy?

This guide explores Deutsche Bank's current product landscape, short-term priorities, mid-term outlook and long-term direction.

## Deutsche Bank's Current Product Landscape

Deutsche Bank reports four core divisions: Corporate Bank, Investment Bank, Private Bank and Asset Management (DWS). Product management roles exist in all four but are concentrated in Corporate Bank and Private Bank technology.

| Division | Key Products | Product Role Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corporate Bank | Cash management, FX4Cash, dbX, trade finance, securities services, Trust and Agency services | Payments platforms, client portals, APIs |
| Investment Bank | Autobahn electronic trading, FX, fixed income platforms | Trading platforms, pricing engines |
| Private Bank | Deutsche Bank and Postbank apps, BestSign, Wero, savings, investments (including maxblue) | Retail apps, authentication, onboarding |
| Asset Management (DWS) | Funds, ETFs (Xtrackers), digital asset initiatives | Fund platforms and distribution |

Strengths:

- One of the world's largest euro clearers, with a dense correspondent banking network.
- Deep relationships with German corporates and the Mittelstand.
- Global network across around 58 countries.
- Large technology centres in Pune, Bangalore, Berlin and Cary.

Weaknesses:

- Legacy systems and complex architecture, illustrated by the 2023 Postbank IT migration problems.
- Historic regulatory and control issues that still shape risk appetite.
- Retail brand perception: Deutsche Bank seen as premium, Postbank as mass-market and associated with service problems.

Strategic Position Matrix:

| Strong Position | Building Position |
| --- | --- |
| Euro clearing and correspondent banking | Digital assets and tokenised money |
| German corporate banking | Mass-market mobile retail (Postbank) |
| FX and cross-currency payments | Mid-cap digital treasury services |

Recent wins and losses:

- Win: dbX consolidated correspondent services under one brand and upgraded FX4Cash for own-currency funding.
- Win: Early mover among German banks on Wero integration.
- Loss: Postbank migration outages damaged customer trust and drew regulatory attention.
- Portfolio change: The bank explored selling its India retail and wealth business in 2025, focusing its India presence on institutional banking and technology centres.

## Short-Term: The Next 12 Months

Priority 1: Real-time and API-first corporate payments

- Scale the Deutsche Bank and Serrala real-time payments API and extend the model to other treasury and ERP vendors.
- Improve straight-through processing as ISO 20022 structured data becomes standard.
- Add Verification of Payee and fraud controls to protect irrevocable instant payments.

Priority 2: Postbank's mobile-first transition

- Support customers affected by branch closures with in-app guidance, video advice and cash access options.
- Improve app reliability and authentication journeys, since trust is the binding constraint.

Priority 3: Wero adoption

- Grow peer-to-peer usage in the Deutsche Bank and Postbank apps and prepare for e-commerce and point-of-sale use cases as EPI expands them.

Priority 4: Efficiency through automation and AI

- Apply AI to operations-heavy processes like trade document checking, payment repairs and client service.
- Consolidate duplicate technology across brands and divisions.

Expected outcomes:

- Higher share of corporate payments sent instantly and straight-through.
- Lower call volumes and churn among Postbank customers.
- Visible cost savings supporting the 2028 return target.

| Initiative | Business Impact | Complexity | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Real-time payments APIs | High | Medium | Ongoing |
| Postbank mobile-first support | High | Medium | Immediate |
| Wero expansion | Medium | Low | Ongoing |
| AI in operations | High | High | Pilots now |

## Mid-Term: 1-5 Year Outlook

Market evolution:

- Instant payments become the default for most euro transfers; batch processing shrinks.
- Tokenised deposits and regulated euro stablecoins move into treasury use cases.
- A digital euro may move closer to launch, changing retail payments and wallets.
- Corporate clients expect embedded banking inside ERP and procurement software.

Strategic moves:

1. Programmable treasury Tokenised euro deposits for intragroup liquidity, then conditional payments and settlement of tokenised funds. Partnerships with stablecoin issuers, including AllUnity, in which DWS is a partner, for open-network access.
2. Mittelstand digital gateway Digital onboarding, transparent FX pricing and subsidiary account opening abroad from one relationship. Digital trade finance with AI-assisted document checks.
3. Unified retail platform Shared front-end components and possibly one app codebase serving both brands. Personalised investing and savings journeys to cross-sell wealth products to Postbank customers.
4. Digital asset services Custody for institutional clients through the Taurus partnership, subject to regulatory approval.

Potential challenges:

- Regulatory uncertainty around tokenised money and capital treatment.
- Competition from J.P. Morgan's Kinexys, Citi Token Services and fintechs like Wise Business.
- Execution risk in large technology programmes across many locations.

## Long-Term: 5-10 Year Projection

Industry shifts:

- Money increasingly moves on shared ledgers, with banks providing regulated tokenised deposits alongside central bank digital currencies.
- Correspondent banking evolves into interoperable networks with 24/7 settlement.
- AI agents initiate a growing share of payments and treasury decisions on behalf of businesses and consumers.
- European banking consolidation continues, with pressure on scale.

Deutsche Bank's potential positioning:

- The leading euro settlement bank for programmable money, extending its clearing strength into tokenised infrastructure.
- A platform provider for corporate treasuries, embedded in ERP and procurement systems.
- A leaner, digital retail bank in Germany with one platform and clear brand roles.

Bold predictions:

1. A majority of Deutsche Bank's euro corporate payments run on instant or tokenised rails.
2. AI handles most routine trade and payment operations, with humans focused on exceptions.
3. Postbank becomes primarily an app-based brand with limited physical presence.

## Strategic Recommendations

Immediate priorities:

1. Make reliability a product feature: Invest in monitoring, release governance and incident response, especially for retail apps and instant payments.
2. Scale APIs through partners: Prioritise treasury and ERP vendor integrations to reach mid-caps cheaply.
3. Protect deposits in a tokenised world: Lead with tokenised deposits for existing clients rather than a standalone stablecoin.

Key decision points:

- Single app versus separate brand apps for Deutsche Bank and Postbank.
- Build versus partner for tokenised money infrastructure.
- How much to automate trade and payment operations with AI under the EU AI Act.

Risk mitigation:

- Phase migrations gradually and keep customers' visible experience stable.
- Engage regulators early on digital assets and AI governance.
- Balance pricing competition with fintechs against bundle value.

> Interview Angle
> When asked about Deutsche Bank's strategy, connect any product idea to the Global Hausbank goal and to capital-light growth. Interviewers expect candidates to understand that payments, FX and cash management are central to the bank's 2028 targets.

## Key Takeaways

1. Deutsche Bank's 2026 strategy centres on scaling the Global Hausbank: capital-light growth in payments, cash management, FX and wealth.
2. Real-time and API-first payments are the immediate product battleground, with dbX, FX4Cash and the Serrala real-time API as key assets.
3. Postbank's mobile-first shift is both a cost lever and a trust challenge.
4. Tokenised money is a long-term opportunity to extend euro clearing leadership.
5. For PM candidates: Show domain knowledge, risk awareness and an understanding of how product decisions connect to return on equity.

Further questions to explore:

- How will a digital euro affect bank apps and deposits?
- Can Deutsche Bank match US banks' investment in corporate digital platforms?
- What role will the India technology centres play in AI-driven operations?
