## Executive Summary

Nubank is Latin America's largest digital bank and one of the most valuable financial institutions in the region. Three factors drive its success: 1) a radically simple, no-fee, mobile-first experience that won customers from Brazil's concentrated, expensive banking system; 2) an extremely low cost to serve, with an efficiency ratio around 20%, which lets it profit from customers incumbents ignored; and 3) a data-driven credit engine that turned millions of first-time borrowers into profitable customers.

Its unique value proposition is to "fight complexity to empower people in their daily lives by reinventing financial services". In practice, that means one app for account, Pix, cards, savings, investments, loans, insurance and even a mobile phone plan.

By mid-2026, Nu Holdings served about 139 million customers, reported quarterly revenue near US$5.9 billion and net income above US$1 billion, became a bank in Mexico, and received conditional US approval for a national bank. This guide examines its current products and how its strategy may evolve over the next decade.

## Introduction

Founded in 2013 by David Vélez, Cristina Junqueira and Edward Wible, Nubank launched with a purple credit card without annual fees. It then added NuConta (a digital account), personal loans, insurance, investments (after acquiring Easynvest, now NuInvest), and expanded to Mexico and Colombia. It listed on the New York Stock Exchange in December 2021.

Key numbers (Q2 2026, as reported):

- About 139 million customers: roughly 118 million in Brazil, 15.8 million in Mexico and more than 5 million in Colombia.
- Revenue of nearly US$5.9 billion in the quarter, up 39% year over year.
- Net income of about US$1.1 billion.
- Deposits of about US$45.3 billion.

The competitive landscape includes large incumbents (Itaú, Bradesco, Banco do Brasil, Caixa, Santander), digital challengers (Inter, C6 Bank, PicPay, PagBank) and ecosystem players (Mercado Pago). In Mexico, BBVA México, Banorte, Mercado Pago, Klar and Stori compete; in Colombia, Bancolombia and Nequi, Davivienda and Lulo Bank.

This guide covers Nubank's current product landscape and its likely priorities in the short, medium and long term.

## Nubank's Current Product Landscape

| Category | Products | Strategic role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Everyday banking | NuConta, Pix, tap-to-pay Pix, debit | Daily engagement, principality |
| Credit | Purple card, Ultravioleta, Pix no Crédito, personal loans, payroll loans (consignado), FGTS anticipation | Main revenue engine |
| Savings and investing | Caixinhas, NuInvest (CDBs, Treasury, funds, stocks) | Deposits and wealth relationships |
| Payments | NuPay online checkout | Owning the checkout button |
| Premium | Ultravioleta (Mastercard Black, 1.25% cashback, zero IOF abroad, lounge) | High-income customers |
| Lifestyle | Nubank+ (with Max streaming), NuCel, NuTravel | Engagement beyond finance |
| Business | Nubank PJ accounts, Charging Assistant for small businesses | SMB banking |
| International | Nu México (bank since August 2026), Nu Colombia, US (Nubank, N.A. in process) | Growth beyond Brazil |
| AI | nuFormer transaction model, AI assistant for Pix | Better underwriting and service |

**Strengths:** brand love, scale, low cost, credit data and models, product speed.

**Weaknesses:** high exposure to consumer credit cycles in Brazil, smaller presence among high-income customers, growing regulatory scrutiny as it becomes systemically important.

**How the pieces fit together:** NuConta and Pix create daily engagement and data; that data feeds credit models (nuFormer) that decide limits and loan offers; credit generates most of the revenue; Caixinhas and NuInvest bring deposits that fund lending cheaply; and lifestyle products such as Nubank+ and NuCel give customers more reasons to open the app every day. Each new product makes the others more valuable, which is the core of Nubank's flywheel.

## Short-Term: The Next 12 Months

**1. Make Nu México a full bank.** Payroll portability, salary-linked benefits, higher deposit insurance and broader credit products. Mexico's low loan-to-deposit ratio (about 35%) leaves room to lend responsibly.

**2. Deepen principality in Brazil.** With most Brazilian adults already customers, growth must come from more products per customer: salary deposits, investments, insurance, payroll loans and Ultravioleta.

**3. Scale AI.** Expand nuFormer to lending in Brazil and cards in Mexico; grow the AI assistant beyond Pix to service and financial guidance; personalise offers.

**4. Responsible credit growth.** Keep delinquency stable while growing secured and payroll lending, which carry lower risk than unsecured cards.

**5. Prepare the US launch.** Complete regulatory approvals, build compliance, and design the first products.

| Initiative | Goal | Key metric |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nu México payroll | Primary relationships | Payroll customers |
| Brazil principality | Revenue per customer | Products per active customer |
| AI underwriting | Better risk decisions | Approval rate at constant losses |
| Secured lending | Lower-risk growth | Payroll and secured loan balances |
| US preparation | Successful launch | Approvals, launch readiness |

## Mid-Term: 1-5 Year Outlook

**Becoming the main bank for more segments.** Nubank's historical strength is the mass market. Over the next five years it will push upmarket through Ultravioleta and NuInvest, and into small businesses with Nubank PJ.

**Platform and ecosystem.** NuPay, Nubank+, NuCel and NuTravel suggest a super-app direction where Nubank becomes part of customers' daily lives. The challenge is to add services without losing simplicity.

**International expansion.** Mexico could become a second Brazil, while Colombia grows steadily. The US is a high-risk, high-reward bet, probably starting with Latin American communities and cross-border needs.

**Technology advantage.** A single global platform and AI models trained on billions of transactions could lower costs further and improve credit decisions across countries.

**Regulation.** As a licensed bank in Brazil and Mexico, Nubank faces higher capital and compliance requirements, but also gains credibility and access to more products.

**Small businesses.** Brazil and Mexico have millions of micro and small businesses that are poorly served by banks. Nubank PJ accounts, tools such as the Charging Assistant for cash flow, NuPay for online sales and tap-to-pay Pix for in-person payments could make Nubank the default bank for entrepreneurs, many of whom already use Nubank personally.

## Long-Term: 5-10 Year Projection

- **Pan-Latin American financial platform.** Nubank could operate in several more Latin American countries, using one platform and shared AI models.
- **AI-first banking.** Customers may interact mainly through AI assistants that manage bills, savings and credit automatically.
- **Wealth management for the mass market.** As customers grow older and wealthier, NuInvest could become a major asset manager.
- **US presence.** If successful, the US could become a meaningful third market, serving Hispanic and underbanked Americans.
- **Risks.** Credit cycles, political and regulatory changes, competition from big tech and ecosystem players, and the complexity of running a global bank.

| Long-term move | Rationale |
| --- | --- |
| New Latin American markets | Reuse platform and brand |
| AI financial assistant | Lower service costs, better outcomes |
| Wealth platform | Higher-value relationships |
| SMB banking at scale | Large underserved segment |

## Strategic Recommendations

1. **Win principality, not just customers.** Use payroll portability, investments and premium offers to become customers' main bank.
2. **Grow credit responsibly.** Shift mix towards secured and payroll lending, use AI to price risk, and invest in financial health features such as emergency funds.
3. **Make Mexico the second engine.** Treat the banking licence as a chance to replicate Brazil's playbook with deposits and payroll.
4. **Enter the US with a focused wedge.** Start with cross-border families and thin-file customers rather than a broad neobank launch.
5. **Keep simplicity as the brand.** As the product range grows, protect the clarity that made Nubank loved.

Risk mitigation:

- **Credit risk:** diversified lending, conservative limit growth, strong monitoring.
- **Regulatory risk:** proactive engagement, strong compliance teams.
- **Execution risk:** stage-gated international investments.

## Key Takeaways

Nubank's future depends on three moves:

1. Turning its huge Brazilian customer base into deeper, multi-product relationships.
2. Making Nu México a full-scale bank with payroll and deposits.
3. Entering the US carefully while maintaining credit discipline and simplicity.

Key metrics to watch: products per customer, Mexico payroll and deposit growth, delinquency trends, efficiency ratio and US launch milestones.

Bottom line: Nubank has proven that a digital bank can be both loved and highly profitable in Latin America. The next decade will test whether it can become the main bank for every segment, in more countries, without losing the simplicity and efficiency that made it successful.
