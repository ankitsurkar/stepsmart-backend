## Executive Summary

In 2026, Deloitte is the world's largest professional services firm, with US$74.5 billion in revenue for the fiscal year ended 31 May 2026 (up 5.7% in US dollars and 3.8% in local currency) and nearly 500,000 people. Behind the headline growth is a strategic challenge: its technology and transformation consulting business grew only about 2.5%, as AI changes how clients buy and how much labour projects need. Deloitte's response rests on three shifts:

1. **From hours to products:** building and selling its own software and AI platforms, led by Zora AI, alongside Omnia for audit and Ascend for delivery.
2. **From people-heavy delivery to AI-powered delivery:** a US$3 billion investment through 2030 to modernise delivery and operations across all businesses.
3. **From offshore delivery to global product capability:** using Deloitte USI and other centres to build and run products, not only execute projects.

The core strategic question is whether a partnership built on billable expertise can also become a successful software company, without losing client trust or the quality that defines its brand.

## Introduction

Deloitte's launch of **Zora AI** in 2025, an agentic AI platform built with NVIDIA, marked a clear change in strategy. Rather than only advising clients on AI, Deloitte now offers pre-built, role-specific agents for finance, procurement, supply chain, customer and human capital work, available by subscription on major clouds and planned to work with SAP's Joule agents.

The market around Deloitte is shifting quickly:

- Clients expect AI to reduce the cost of consulting work and are pushing for outcome-based pricing.
- Software vendors (SAP, Salesforce, Microsoft, ServiceNow, Workday) are bundling AI agents into their platforms.
- Rival firms, including Accenture, PwC, EY and KPMG, are making similar moves into AI platforms.
- Public cases of AI errors in professional work, including Deloitte Australia's partial refund of a government report in 2025, have raised the bar for quality and transparency.

Key strategic questions:

1. Can Deloitte build software products that clients buy on their own merits?
2. How does it protect consulting revenue while AI shrinks the hours needed?
3. How does it scale AI while keeping trust, independence and quality?

## Deloitte's Current Product Landscape

Deloitte's business remains mostly services. Its revenue broadly comes from consulting and technology transformation, audit and assurance, tax and legal, and advisory services (strategy, risk and transactions). In FY2026, tax and legal was the fastest-growing business (about 5.8%), followed by audit and assurance (about 5%), while technology and transformation lagged at about 2.5%. Asia Pacific was the fastest-growing region at about 7.5%.

Its product and platform portfolio includes:

- **Zora AI:** agentic AI platform with function-specific agents; the flagship external product
- **Omnia and Levvia:** cloud audit platforms used by Deloitte's audit teams globally
- **Ascend:** platform for consulting delivery, with product owners hired in USI
- **Internal AI tools:** generative AI assistants such as PairD for practitioners
- **Deloitte Digital assets:** accelerators for customer experience, marketing and commerce

Recent wins and challenges:

- **Win:** Strong alliances with NVIDIA, SAP, Google Cloud, AWS, Microsoft and Salesforce give Deloitte access to the largest enterprise technology deals.
- **Win:** Consistent growth in audit and tax, which fund investment.
- **Challenge:** Slow consulting growth as clients delay large transformation programmes.
- **Challenge:** Reputational risk from AI quality issues in client work.

Strategic Position Matrix:

|  | Strong position | Building position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Services | Audit, tax, large ERP and cloud transformations | Outcome-based managed services |
| Products | Omnia (internal audit platform) | Zora AI, Ascend, AI agents for clients |

## Short-Term: The Next 12 Months

1. **Prove Zora AI value in production** Move clients from pilots to production subscriptions in finance and procurement Build standard packages and clear pricing Success metric: pilot-to-production conversion and live production workflows
2. **AI-powered delivery across engagements** Use AI tools to speed research, documentation, coding and testing Strengthen review and verification standards for AI-assisted deliverables Success metric: productivity gains with zero major AI quality incidents
3. **Deepen platform alliances** Integrate Zora AI with SAP Joule; strengthen work with NVIDIA, cloud providers and Salesforce Success metric: co-sold deals and joint client wins

Strategic dialogue — three questions observers ask:

1. **Will AI shrink Deloitte's consulting revenue?** Some routine work will shrink, but demand for AI implementation, change management and governance is rising. The firm's bet is that it can capture more value per engagement with fewer hours.
2. **Can a consulting firm sell software?** It requires product discipline, standard pricing and incentive changes for partners, which are harder than the technology.
3. **How will Deloitte handle AI quality risk?** By building review standards, citation checks and governance into its tools, and by being transparent with clients.

## Mid-Term: 1-5 Year Outlook

Strategic bets:

1. **Digital workforce:** AI agents performing parts of finance, procurement, HR and customer operations for clients, sold by subscription or outcome.
2. **Managed services with AI:** Deloitte running business processes for clients, using agents plus people, with outcome-based contracts.
3. **Product engineering at scale in USI:** India-based teams owning and running products, not only delivering projects.

Build vs. buy:

- **Build:** function-specific agents, governance tooling, industry solutions
- **Partner:** AI models and infrastructure (NVIDIA, cloud providers), enterprise platforms (SAP and others)
- **Buy:** possible acquisitions of AI and data firms to add capabilities quickly (speculative)

Market entries and exits:

- **Entry:** AI managed services, AI assurance (auditing AI systems), sovereign cloud and AI offerings in Europe
- **Possible narrowing:** low-value, labour-heavy services that AI commoditises

Strategic Framework Analysis:

📌 **Where to Play:** large enterprises and governments undergoing AI-driven transformation in finance, supply chain, customer and workforce functions.

📌 **How to Win:** combine trusted relationships, deep functional and industry expertise, alliances with technology leaders and a growing portfolio of AI products.

📌 **Why Now:** enterprises are moving from AI pilots to production, and they need both technology and change management at scale.

## Long-Term: 5-10 Year Projection

Core assumptions:

1. Much routine knowledge work will be done by AI agents supervised by people.
2. Clients will pay more for outcomes and less for hours.
3. Trust, assurance and accountability will become more valuable as AI spreads.

Major technology bets:

- **Agentic enterprise platforms:** Zora AI evolving into an orchestration layer across many systems.
- **AI assurance:** auditing and certifying AI systems, a natural extension of Deloitte's audit heritage.
- **Industry clouds and solutions:** packaged solutions for sectors such as health, financial services and government.

Potential disruption factors:

- **Software vendors capture the value** by building agents into their own platforms.
- **AI-native consultancies** with far lower cost structures.
- **Regulation** of AI use in audit, public sector and regulated industries.

Observer views (paraphrased):

- Industry analysts often argue that the Big Four's strongest long-term advantage in AI is trust and assurance rather than technology.
- Others note that partnership structures and billable-hour incentives make it hard for professional services firms to build software businesses.

## Strategic Recommendations

1. **Run Zora AI like a product company:** standard packages, transparent pricing, product leadership with decision rights, and incentives that reward software revenue.
2. **Make trust a product feature:** build verification, audit trails and governance into every AI tool and deliverable.
3. **Shift to outcome-based offerings:** use AI agents plus people to sell measurable outcomes in finance and operations.
4. **Build product capability in USI:** product career tracks, product academies and ownership of selected products.
5. **Stay platform-neutral where it matters:** integrate deeply with SAP and others while keeping Zora AI's cross-platform role.

Success metrics to watch:

- Zora AI production clients and recurring revenue
- Consulting growth rate versus peers
- Share of revenue from outcome-based and subscription contracts
- AI quality incidents and client trust scores

Key risks and mitigations:

- **Incentive conflict:** align partner rewards with product revenue
- **Quality incidents:** mandatory verification and accountability
- **Vendor competition:** focus on cross-platform orchestration and expertise

Timeline of expected shifts:

- Year 1: Zora AI moves from pilots to production; AI-assisted delivery standardised
- Years 2–3: outcome-based managed services scale; USI owns more product development
- Years 3–5: software and outcome-based revenue becomes a significant share of consulting

## Key Takeaways

Deloitte's future depends on three moves:

1. Turning AI from a threat to its hours-based model into a source of new products and outcome-based revenue.
2. Making Zora AI a product clients choose on its own merits, not only as part of an engagement.
3. Protecting the trust and quality that its brand depends on as AI becomes part of every deliverable.

For PM candidates, strong interview answers connect product ideas to these themes: client outcomes, trust and governance, platform partnerships, and the shift from services to scalable products.

Bottom Line: Deloitte has the relationships, expertise and capital to lead enterprise AI adoption. The open question is whether it can build the product discipline to turn that advantage into durable software businesses.
