## Executive Summary

In 2026 Epic Systems is the dominant US EHR vendor, and its strategy is shifting from "the system of record" to "the system of intelligence". Three moves define it:

1. AI at the centre: At its August 2026 user group meeting, Epic focused on AI agents. Art (for clinicians), Emmie (for patients) and Penny (for revenue cycle) are in use across hundreds of organisations, and Agent Factory, a no-code platform to build and run AI agents, is planned for general availability in 2027. Epic reported about 1.4 million clinicians assisted by AI each month.
2. Data as an advantage: Cosmos now covers about 320 million unique patients and 23 billion encounters, powering predictions, research and clinician tools.
3. Faster delivery: Epic moved from releases every 18 months to every three months, getting innovation to customers much faster.

Epic's challenge is not market share but keeping customers happy while moving fast: proving AI value, managing costs for hospitals under financial pressure, and expanding internationally without diluting quality.

## Introduction

Epic was founded in 1979 by Judy Faulkner and remains privately held. It has grown largely without acquisitions, building its products in-house on its Verona, Wisconsin campus. Its customers include many of the largest US health systems and academic medical centres, plus organisations in 17 countries.

Key strategic questions:

1. How can Epic make AI genuinely useful and safe for clinicians and patients, and capture value from it?
2. How should it balance its integrated platform with an ecosystem of third-party AI and app vendors?
3. Where should it grow next: international markets, payers, life sciences or new care settings?
4. How can it help financially stressed customers get more value from their Epic investment?

## Epic Systems' Current Product Landscape

| Product | What it does | Strategic role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hyperspace / Haiku / Rover | Clinical workspace (desktop and mobile) | Core of clinician experience |
| MyChart | Patient portal and app, including Fast Pass, messaging, billing, Emmie | Patient engagement, digital front door |
| Cosmos | De-identified dataset of about 320 million patients | Research, AI, benchmarking |
| Art | AI for clinicians: summaries, In Basket drafting, documentation | Reduce burnout, increase productivity |
| Emmie | AI for patients in MyChart | Reduce call centre and message load |
| Penny | AI for revenue cycle | Coding, billing, denials |
| Agent Factory | No-code AI agent platform (GA 2027) | Platform for health system AI |
| Community Connect | Lets large systems extend Epic to smaller hospitals and clinics | Reach into community settings |
| Interoperability (Care Everywhere, TEFCA) | Exchange of records | Network effects |

Strengths:

- Integrated suite and data model across clinical, patient and financial workflows.
- High customer satisfaction and strong user community.
- Massive data through Cosmos and Care Everywhere: about 9.3 billion patient records exchanged last year, half with non-Epic systems.

Weaknesses:

- High cost and long implementations.
- Dependence on customers' capacity to adopt features.
- Perception as a closed ecosystem.

## Short-Term: The Next 12 Months

1. **Prove AI value**: Publish results like Texas Children's 50% reduction in time reviewing patient messages and Community Health Network's 73% fewer billing-related In Basket messages; expand Art summaries (already at 300+ health systems) and Chart with Art across more specialties.
2. **Agent Factory early adopters**: Work with early customers on templates, governance and monitoring before 2027 general availability.
3. **Quarterly release adoption**: Help customers turn on valuable features faster through recommendations and pre-built configurations.
4. **MyChart growth**: Scale Emmie, MyChart Central (live at 346 organisations covering 115 million patients) and AI result explanations.
5. **International go-lives**: Deliver the Charité implementation in Germany successfully.

| Initiative | Expected outcome | Metric |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI value proof | More AI adoption | Clinicians using AI monthly, validated time saved |
| Agent Factory pilots | Platform readiness | Agents in production at early adopters |
| Feature adoption tools | Faster time to value | Value-weighted feature adoption |
| MyChart and Emmie | Lower patient service costs | Safely resolved patient requests |
| Charité | Reference site in Europe | On-time go-live, user satisfaction |

> Interview Angle
> When discussing Epic's priorities, connect AI to customer finances. Health systems buy AI to reduce burnout, staffing costs and revenue leakage, not for novelty.

## Mid-Term: 1-5 Year Outlook

1. **Agent ecosystem**: Agent Factory becomes the standard way health systems deploy AI, with Epic-built agents as defaults and certified partners plugging in.
2. **Cosmos-driven care**: Predictions and treatment insights based on Cosmos become routine at the point of care, for example Best Care Choices for My Patient and length-of-stay predictions.
3. **Revenue cycle automation**: Penny and coding assistants reduce denials and manual work; Epic reported 33% fewer coding-related denials across 160 organisations with its Professional Billing Coding Assistant.
4. **Payer and life sciences platforms**: Epic extends its network to payers (prior authorisation, data exchange) and supports clinical research and trial matching.
5. **International growth**: More countries, with localisation and regional hosting.

For product teams, the mid-term shift means more work on platform capabilities (permissions, monitoring, evaluation tools for AI) and less on standalone screens. It also means closer collaboration with customers' AI governance committees, which increasingly decide whether a feature can go live. Developers and quality managers who understand evaluation, bias testing and clinical safety will be especially valuable.

Risks: regulatory scrutiny of AI and data practices, competition from Oracle Health's new AI-first EHR and big tech AI platforms, and customer cost pressures.

## Long-Term: 5-10 Year Projection

- **Ambient, agent-driven care**: Much of documentation, ordering preparation, scheduling and billing done by agents under clinician supervision.
- **Learning health system at national scale**: Cosmos enabling near real-time evidence on what works, feeding back into care decisions.
- **Patient-centred records**: MyChart as a lifelong health companion across organisations and countries.
- **Global platform**: Epic as a major health IT platform in several international markets.

| Scenario | Description | Implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base case | Steady AI adoption, gradual international growth | Epic consolidates leadership |
| Upside | Agents dramatically cut admin costs | Epic becomes essential AI platform for healthcare |
| Downside | AI safety incidents or regulation slow adoption | Focus shifts to governance and core EHR quality |

## Strategic Recommendations

1. **Measure AI by outcomes**: Track validated time saved, denials avoided and patient outcomes, and publish them.
2. **Make governance a product**: Built-in testing, monitoring and audit for every AI agent will win health system AI committees.
3. **Open selectively**: Certify partner agents and offer controlled Cosmos validation to strengthen the ecosystem without losing trust.
4. **Reduce customer effort**: More features on by default, pre-built configurations and recommendations per organisation.
5. **Expand internationally with lighthouse sites**: Depth before breadth, starting with Germany.

> For Candidates
> Epic interviewers care about customers and patients more than market jargon. Frame strategy answers around helping clinicians and patients, then show the business effect.

## Key Takeaways

- Epic is moving from EHR leader to AI and data platform.
- Agent Factory, Art, Emmie and Penny define its AI strategy; general availability of Agent Factory is planned for 2027.
- Cosmos is a unique data advantage that must be managed carefully.
- Quarterly releases speed innovation but increase pressure on customer adoption.
- International expansion is selective, with Charité as a key European reference.

Bottom line: Epic's next decade depends on turning its data and workflow dominance into AI that measurably reduces burden and improves care, while keeping the trust of customers who own its data and run its software.
