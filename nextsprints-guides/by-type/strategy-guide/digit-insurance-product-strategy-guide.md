## Executive Summary

In 2026, Digit Insurance is moving from a fast-growing insurtech challenger to a listed, profit-focused general insurer. Three shifts define its strategy:

1. **From growth to profitable growth:** FY26 gross written premium rose about 9.8% to roughly ₹11,294 crore, while profit after tax rose about 28% to roughly ₹544 crore. Investors now judge Digit on combined ratio and return on equity, not just premium growth.
2. **From channel-based to partner-based underwriting:** At its February 2026 analyst day, Digit described pricing and terms by individual partner, cutting back on lower-return partners and scaling direct premium fetching with vehicle manufacturers.
3. **From motor-heavy to more balanced:** Motor remains the core, with a 6.3% market share in FY26, but the health business declined in FY26, making diversification a priority.

Digit's direction is clear: use technology and partner relationships to grow profitably in motor, rebuild health, and prepare for regulatory changes such as IRDAI's Bima Sugam marketplace.

## Introduction

India's general insurance market is large and underpenetrated, but it is also competitive. Private insurers such as ICICI Lombard, Bajaj General and HDFC ERGO have scale and bank channels; standalone health insurers dominate retail health; and digital players like Acko compete on simplicity.

Digit launched in 2017 with a promise of "simplifying insurance": easy policy language, smartphone-based claims and a strong technology platform for partners. It grew rapidly, mainly through partners, and listed in May 2024.

Key questions this guide explores:

1. How can Digit keep growing in motor while improving profitability?
2. Can Digit rebuild its health business?
3. How should Digit balance partner-led distribution with direct customer relationships?

## Short-Term: The Next 12 Months

**1. Partner-based underwriting at scale**

- Price and set commissions by partner portfolio quality.
- Exit or reprice loss-making partners.
- Success metric: improvement in motor loss ratio while keeping share.

**2. Expand direct premium fetching with OEMs**

- Grow beyond the five OEMs mentioned in early 2026.
- Success metric: share of new-vehicle premium through OEM integrations.

**3. Stabilise health**

- Simplify retail products, push super top-ups and improve cashless claims.
- Success metric: retail health premium growth with claims ratio within target.

**4. Claims experience**

- Faster self-inspection and AI approval for small claims; compliance with IRDAI's cashless health timelines.
- Success metric: claim turnaround time and complaints per 10,000 policies.

**5. Renewal retention**

- Make renewals direct-first through WhatsApp and the app for every customer, with partners credited where agreements require.
- Explain price changes and No Claim Bonus value clearly to reduce switching on aggregators.
- Success metric: motor renewal rate by original channel.

**6. Usage-based motor pilots**

- Test pay-as-you-drive add-ons using kilometre slabs, with optional telematics for safe-driver discounts.
- Success metric: take-up among low-mileage drivers and claim frequency versus standard policies.

What PM candidates should take from this: almost every short-term priority is a product problem, from partner pricing tools to claims automation. Interview answers that connect features to loss ratio and combined ratio will match how Digit's leadership is judged by investors.

## Mid-Term: 1-5 Year Outlook

**Combined ratio below 100%.** The biggest mid-term goal is underwriting profitability, reducing reliance on investment income.

**Balanced portfolio.** Grow health and commercial lines so motor is less dominant, reducing exposure to motor pricing cycles and tariff changes.

**Direct relationship layer.** Even with partner acquisition, Digit can own claims and renewal relationships through its app and WhatsApp.

**Usage-based and embedded insurance.** IRDAI's approval of telematics-based add-ons and growing connected-car adoption open pay-as-you-drive products. Embedded cover with e-commerce, travel and mobility platforms can grow.

**Bima Sugam.** IRDAI's planned digital marketplace could make comparing and buying insurance easier, changing aggregator economics. Digit's API-first systems are an advantage.

Scenarios:

| Scenario | Description | Likelihood |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Profitable leader in motor | Combined ratio below 100%, steady share gains | Medium-High |
| Diversified insurer | Health and commercial lines become a large share | Medium |
| Margin squeeze | Pricing competition and regulation hurt motor profits | Low-Medium |

## Long-Term: 5-10 Year Projection

Over the next decade, Indian general insurance is likely to change in three ways:

1. **Data-driven pricing:** Connected vehicles, health data (with consent) and digital public infrastructure such as account aggregator and the National Health Claims Exchange will enable more precise pricing.
2. **Automated claims:** Most small claims could be settled automatically within minutes.
3. **Open distribution:** Bima Sugam and embedded channels could reduce the power of traditional intermediaries.

Digit's opportunity is to be the insurer best equipped for this world: API-first products, automated claims and partner-level pricing. Its risk is that larger insurers with bank channels and stronger brands catch up on technology.

Potential long-term moves:

- Insurance-as-a-service for platforms (mobility, e-commerce, fintech).
- Fully automated motor claims with OEM data.
- Expansion into life insurance through Digit Life, where the company has signalled plans.

## Strategic Recommendations

1. **Make profitability visible in every product decision.** Track loss ratio and combined ratio guardrails in product experiments.
2. **Own the post-purchase relationship.** Claims and renewals should be direct-first experiences regardless of who sold the policy.
3. **Rebuild health with discipline.** Lead with super top-ups and cross-sell to motor customers; invest in cashless claims before scaling.
4. **Double down on OEM and embedded channels.** These offer lower acquisition costs and better data.
5. **Prepare for open distribution.** Be ready for Bima Sugam with fast APIs and simple products.

| Recommendation | Impact | Effort | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Profitability guardrails | High | Low | Now |
| Direct post-purchase relationship | High | Medium | 0–12 months |
| Health rebuild | High | High | 0–36 months |
| OEM and embedded growth | High | Medium | 0–24 months |
| Bima Sugam readiness | Medium | Medium | 12–36 months |

## Key Takeaways

Digit's future depends on:

1. Achieving underwriting profitability in motor without losing share.
2. Reversing the decline in health.
3. Using technology to make claims and renewals faster and fairer than competitors.

Metrics to watch: combined ratio, motor market share, retail health premium growth, renewal rates and claims turnaround.

Bottom line: Digit has proven that a technology-led, partner-first insurer can scale in India. The next phase is proving it can do so profitably and across more lines.
