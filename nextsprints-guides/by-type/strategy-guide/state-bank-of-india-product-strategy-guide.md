## Executive Summary

In 2026, State Bank of India is trying to do something few large incumbents manage: rebuild its core digital product while serving more than 50 crore customers. SBI reported a record net profit of about ₹80,032 crore for FY26, and almost all its transactions now run through alternate channels. Its digital strategy rests on three shifts:

1. **From YONO to YONO 2.0:** A lighter, faster app that unifies mobile and internet banking, with a goal of doubling users from about 10 crore to 20 crore within two years.
2. **From transactions to relationships:** Using YONO to sell loans, investments and insurance, and to give customers tools such as the Financial Fitness dashboard.
3. **From branches to "phygital":** Using branches and thousands of digital support staff to bring less digital customers online, rather than replacing branches.

SBI's challenge is clear. Its scale and trust are unmatched, but its digital experience has lagged private banks and fintechs. The next two years will show whether YONO 2.0 can turn scale into engagement.

## Introduction

YONO 2.0's launch in December 2025 was SBI's biggest product bet since YONO itself launched in 2017. The bank said it would migrate existing users in phases over six to eight months, hire digital support staff to help, and invest heavily to double users. In July 2026, on its 71st Bank Day, SBI added a 3-in-1 savings, demat and trading account with SBICAP Securities, a Financial Fitness dashboard, a sustainability Green Score, mobile trade finance on YONO Business and YONO Ji, an agentic AI assistant for business users.

This guide looks at SBI's current product landscape, short-term priorities, mid-term outlook and long-term direction, and the strategic questions SBI faces:

1. How can SBI turn registered users into active, loyal digital customers?
2. How should it compete with UPI apps that own daily payments for its own customers?
3. How should it balance growth, inclusion and fraud risk?

## State Bank of India's Current Product Landscape

SBI's digital portfolio spans the bank and its group companies.

| Product | Segment | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YONO / YONO 2.0 | Retail | Main banking, lending and investment app |
| Internet banking (being unified with YONO 2.0) | Retail | Web banking |
| YONO Business | Corporate, MSME | Payments, approvals, trade finance, YONO Ji |
| BHIM SBI Pay | Retail | Standalone UPI app |
| SBI Card app | Cards | Card management; SBI Card holds about 18.6% of cards in force |
| SBI Life, SBI MF, SBICAP Securities apps | Insurance, investments | Group products, increasingly sold through YONO |

Key numbers (public reports, approximate):

- YONO registered users: about 10 crore (FY26).
- Share of savings accounts opened via YONO: about 66% (FY26).
- Alternate channels' share of transactions: about 98.7% (FY26).
- YONO 2.0 user target: 20 crore within two years of December 2025.

Recent wins and losses:

- **Win:** Digital account opening at scale; most new savings accounts now come through YONO.
- **Win:** Pre-approved personal loans that disburse instantly without documents.
- **Loss:** UPI. SBI is the biggest bank behind UPI payments but its own apps start only a small share of them; PhonePe and Google Pay dominate.
- **Loss:** Reputation for app reliability, with past outages and migration complaints.

Strategic position matrix:

|  | Strong position | Weak position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Large market | Deposits, salary and pension accounts, digital account opening | UPI payments, everyday engagement |
| Growing market | Pre-approved lending, credit cards (SBI Card) | Wealth and investing for young users |

## Short-Term: The Next 12 Months

SBI's short-term strategy centres on three themes.

1. **Finishing the YONO 2.0 migration well** Move remaining users from the old app without losing them. Fix login, re-registration and device issues users have raised. Success metric: 95% of active users migrated with stable monthly activity.
2. **Activation, not just registration** Use branches and digital support staff for assisted onboarding. Build simple, vernacular journeys for first-time users. Success metric: higher share of registered users completing at least one task each month.
3. **Deeper cross-sell through YONO** Scale the 3-in-1 account, pre-approved loans, and insurance and investment journeys. Use the Financial Fitness dashboard to make offers more relevant. Success metric: digital share of retail product sales.

Other near-term moves:

- Expanding YONO Business with trade finance and YONO Ji.
- Strengthening fraud controls, especially around mule accounts and social engineering.

> Interview angle
> Expect interviewers to ask how you would prioritise between migration quality and new features. A strong answer protects existing users first, because losing them would erase the gains from new acquisition.

## Mid-Term: 1-5 Year Outlook

Over the next few years, several trends will shape SBI's product strategy:

- **AI in banking:** From YONO Ji in business banking to assistants in retail YONO that answer questions, explain spending and handle service requests.
- **Credit on UPI and new-to-credit growth:** SBI and SBI Card can use bank data to offer small-ticket credit safely, especially in smaller cities.
- **Account Aggregator and open data:** Financial Fitness can grow into a view of a customer's money across banks.
- **Digital rupee and new rails:** SBI's role in pilots of the RBI's digital currency and in cross-border UPI links.
- **MSME digitisation:** YONO Business as a working capital and trade platform for small businesses.

Strategic options:

| Option | Benefit | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Make YONO a super-app with lifestyle partners | Daily engagement | Earlier "lifestyle" efforts had limited traction |
| Focus YONO on core banking excellence | Reliability and trust | Less engagement between transactions |
| Partner with UPI apps for distribution | Reach | Loss of customer ownership |
| Build SBI-owned payments and credit habits | Data and relationship | Costly against big tech |

A likely path combines strong core banking with selected financial tools (credit, investing, insurance) rather than broad lifestyle features.

## Long-Term: 5-10 Year Projection

In the long run, SBI could become India's leading digital public bank, combining:

- A YONO used daily by most of its customers, in their own language, with AI help.
- Branches focused on advice, complex products and inclusion, not routine transactions.
- A platform for MSMEs that covers payments, credit and trade.
- Group companies (cards, insurance, funds, broking) served through one customer relationship.

Potential disruptors:

- Big tech and fintech platforms offering deposits through partner banks.
- AI agents managing customers' money across institutions.
- Regulatory changes in data sharing and digital lending.

SBI's advantages, including trust, deposits, government relationships and reach, will matter, but only if its digital experience is competitive.

## Strategic Recommendations

1. **Define success as active users.** Measure YONO 2.0 by monthly active, self-served customers, not registrations.
2. **Protect, then grow.** Prioritise migration quality and reliability before adding features.
3. **Win the next 10 crore with phygital onboarding.** Use branches, Business Correspondents and digital support staff, plus simple vernacular journeys.
4. **Compete in payments on bank-only strengths.** Real-time account visibility, faster refunds and credit on UPI rather than cashback wars.
5. **Personalise responsibly.** Use Financial Fitness to guide product offers, with strong guardrails against over-borrowing.
6. **Make YONO Business an MSME platform.** Build on trade finance and YONO Ji to deepen corporate and small business relationships.

## Key Takeaways

1. YONO 2.0 is SBI's central digital bet, with a goal of 20 crore users within two years.
2. SBI's scale and trust are strong, but its apps must match private bank and fintech experiences.
3. The biggest growth pool is less digital, more rural and more vernacular customers.
4. Payments engagement is a weak spot; credit and bank-only features are the way back.
5. AI, credit on UPI and open data will shape the next phase.

Key metrics to watch: YONO monthly active users, migration completion, digital share of product sales, payment share in SBI apps, and fraud losses.

Bottom line: SBI has the customers, the balance sheet and the trust. Its product strategy will succeed if it turns those assets into an everyday digital habit.
