## Executive Summary

In 2026 Harvey is the most prominent AI company in legal services and is moving from a tool lawyers use into a platform where legal work gets done. Three strategic shifts define its direction:

1. From assistant to agents: Harvey Agents now execute multi-step legal tasks end to end, with lawyers supervising rather than doing each step.
2. From law firms to the whole legal ecosystem: in-house legal teams, mid-size firms, firm-client collaboration through Shared Spaces, and partnerships with content providers such as LexisNexis.
3. From legal to professional services: work with firms such as PwC and "New Verticals" product roles point to expansion into tax and adjacent fields.

Harvey reported more than 100,000 lawyers across over 1,300 organisations in early 2026 and annualised revenue of roughly USD 190 million in January 2026, with later third-party estimates higher. It raised at a reported USD 11 billion valuation in March 2026. Its strategy is to become the operating system for legal work while staying the highest-quality option in a crowded market.

## Introduction

Legal AI is one of the most competitive categories in enterprise AI. Thomson Reuters (CoCounsel and Westlaw) and LexisNexis (Lexis+ AI) own legal content; startups such as Legora compete for the same law firms; practice management vendors such as Clio add AI features; and general tools like Microsoft Copilot sit on every lawyer's desktop. Harvey's response has been to move fast, focus on quality, and build a broad platform.

Key strategic questions:

1. How can Harvey keep its quality lead as foundation models become widely available?
2. How should it price agents as they replace hours of work?
3. Should it expand beyond legal, and when?

This guide reviews Harvey's current products, its likely short, mid and long-term moves, and recommendations PM candidates can use in strategy interviews.

## Harvey's Current Product Landscape

Estimated revenue mix (inferred, not company-disclosed):

- Large law firms: largest share
- Corporate in-house legal teams: fast-growing
- Professional services firms and mid-size firms: smaller but growing

| Product | What it does | Strategic role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assistant | Chat for drafting, analysis and questions | Daily entry point |
| Vault | Secure storage and bulk analysis of large document sets | High-value projects like diligence |
| Knowledge | Legal, regulatory and tax research with citations | Research habit; content partnerships |
| Workflows | Pre-built and custom multi-step processes | Encodes firm know-how; stickiness |
| Agents | End-to-end execution of legal tasks with review | Next growth engine |
| Shared Spaces | Collaboration between firms and clients | Network effects |
| Word add-in and Microsoft 365 integration | Harvey inside lawyers' daily tools | Habit and retention |

> Strategic Insight
> Harvey's most defensible asset may be the tens of thousands of custom workflows and agents customers have built. Each one encodes a firm's own expertise and makes switching harder.

Key partnerships and integrations:

- LexisNexis alliance bringing primary law content and citation tools into Harvey.
- Microsoft 365 integration (2026) and a Connector Library linking to firms' systems.
- Long-standing relationships with global firms and professional services firms.

## Short-Term: The Next 12 Months

Priority 1: Make agents reliable and supervised

- Agents for diligence, contract review, research memos and litigation preparation.
- Adaptive review checkpoints and clear audit trails for supervising lawyers.

Priority 2: Deepen daily habit

- Stronger Word and Microsoft 365 integration so Harvey works where lawyers draft.
- Faster, more accurate Knowledge research with visible citation validation.

Priority 3: Expand beyond the largest firms

- Packages, onboarding and practice-area workflow packs for mid-size firms and in-house teams.

Priority 4: Pricing for agents

- Hybrid models combining seats with agent usage that can be attributed to client matters.

| Initiative | Expected impact | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supervised agents | Larger share of legal work | Errors in high-stakes tasks |
| Microsoft 365 depth | Daily active usage | Dependence on Microsoft's platform |
| Mid-size packaging | More customers | Lower deal sizes, support load |
| Agent pricing | Revenue tied to value | Customer budget concerns |

## Mid-Term: 1-5 Year Outlook

1. Billing model shift: as agents do more work, clients will push law firms further toward fixed fees. Harvey can help firms price and deliver fixed-fee work profitably, positioning itself as a partner in the business model change rather than a threat to billable hours.
2. Firm-client networks: Shared Spaces could connect firms and their clients on one platform. If both sides use Harvey, collaboration becomes smoother and switching becomes harder for both.
3. Professional services expansion: tax, compliance and regulatory work share Harvey's core patterns (documents, research, accountable experts). Expansion is likely to start through existing customers.
4. Global growth: legal systems differ by country, so expansion requires jurisdiction-specific content, models and workflows. Europe and Asia-Pacific offer large markets with fewer AI-native competitors.

> Key Risk
> Content owners are also competitors. Thomson Reuters and LexisNexis own much of the primary legal content. Harvey's partnership strategy must balance access to content with the risk that partners build competing products.

## Long-Term: 5-10 Year Projection

Scenario 1: Operating system for legal work (bull case) Most legal work at large firms and corporations flows through Harvey: intake, research, drafting, review, collaboration and billing insights.

Scenario 2: Leading premium legal AI among several platforms Harvey remains the top-quality option for complex work, while incumbents and bundled tools take routine work.

Scenario 3: Commoditisation If general AI assistants become good enough for most legal tasks, Harvey must rely on workflows, integrations and trust to justify premium pricing.

Signals to watch that would show which scenario is unfolding:

- Share of Harvey usage coming from agents rather than chat.
- Whether large firms renew with expanded seat counts or shift spend to bundled tools.
- Pricing pressure in head-to-head deals against Thomson Reuters and Legora.
- Adoption of Shared Spaces by in-house teams that are not already Harvey customers.

Long-term product bets:

- Autonomous agents for whole matter types, such as standard commercial contracting.
- Predictive tools for litigation and negotiation using aggregated, consented data.
- Platform marketplaces where firms share or sell workflows.

## Strategic Recommendations

1. Keep quality as the brand. Invest in evaluations, citation validation and expert audits. In legal, one high-profile error costs more than many features gain.
2. Lead the agent transition with supervision built in. Design agents that make lawyer oversight easy and visible, aligning with professional duties.
3. Own the daily workflow. Deep integration with Word, Outlook and document management systems makes Harvey part of every task.
4. Help customers change business models. Tools for fixed-fee pricing and matter-level AI cost attribution make Harvey an ally to firms.
5. Expand adjacently and carefully. Start with tax and compliance through existing customers, with strict limits on investment until traction is proven.

| Recommendation | Timeline | Owner area |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quality and evaluation investment | Ongoing | Knowledge and platform PMs |
| Supervised agents | 0-12 months | Agents PMs |
| Workflow integration | 0-18 months | Core platform PMs |
| Business model tools | 6-24 months | Commercial and analytics PMs |
| Adjacent verticals | 12-36 months | New Verticals PMs |

## Key Takeaways

1. Harvey is moving from AI assistant to platform and agents for legal work.
2. Quality and trust are its main competitive advantages in a crowded market.
3. Custom workflows and agents create switching costs.
4. Pricing and billing models will change as agents do more work.
5. Expansion into professional services is likely, but legal remains the core.

For PM candidates: in strategy interviews, connect product ideas to lawyers' accountability, firms' economics and Harvey's need to stay the highest-quality option.
