## Executive Summary

In 2026, Tata Consultancy Services is in the middle of its most important strategic shift since it became India's largest IT services company. Revenue growth has slowed to low single digits, clients are using AI to cut the very effort that services firms bill for, and hyperscalers and AI labs are moving closer to enterprise customers. TCS's response has three parts:

1. **AI-led services repositioning:** A "Human+AI" operating model, AI services revenue of about US$2.3 billion annualised in FY26, and agentic AI offerings across industries.
2. **Infrastructure-to-intelligence play:** HyperVault, an AI data-centre subsidiary incorporated in October 2025 with an ambition of 1 GW capacity, backed by partnerships with OpenAI and AMD and a reported US$1 billion investment from TPG.
3. **Products and platforms as differentiators:** TCS BaNCS, ignio, TCS iON, MasterCraft, Optumera and WisdomNext give TCS intellectual property that pure services rivals lack.

For product managers, the key point is that TCS's platforms are moving from "supporting tools for services deals" to "the core of how TCS wins and keeps clients". That raises the importance of product thinking across the company.

## Introduction

For decades, TCS grew by supplying skilled engineers at scale to global enterprises. Its products were important but small relative to the services engine. Generative AI changes the economics: if AI can write, test and maintain much of the code that clients used to pay people for, TCS needs new ways to capture value.

Key strategic questions for TCS:

1. How can TCS grow revenue when AI reduces billable effort per project?
2. Should TCS invest more in owned products and platforms, or remain primarily a services and integration partner?
3. How should TCS compete with hyperscalers, AI labs and consulting firms for the enterprise AI budget?
4. How does an infrastructure bet like HyperVault fit with an asset-light services company?

This guide looks at TCS's current product landscape, its likely moves over the next 12 months, the mid-term outlook and a long-term projection, followed by recommendations relevant to PM candidates.

## TCS's Current Product Landscape

TCS does not report detailed revenue by product, but its portfolio can be grouped as follows (shares are rough estimates):

- **Services (consulting, application development and maintenance, infrastructure, BPS):** about 90–95% of revenue
- **Products and platforms (BaNCS, ignio, iON, MasterCraft, Optumera, others):** about 5–10% of revenue, but with higher strategic value through client stickiness

| Product | Domain | Strategic role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TCS BaNCS | Core banking, payments, capital markets, insurance | Deep, multi-decade client relationships; Gartner MQ leader for retail core banking in Europe (2025) |
| ignio (Digitate) | AIOps and autonomous operations | Differentiates managed-services deals; AI credibility |
| TCS iON | Exams, assessments, learning | Strong India presence; NQT feeds TCS's own hiring |
| TCS MasterCraft (TransformPlus, DataPlus) | Legacy modernisation, data privacy | Accelerates mainframe-to-cloud programmes |
| TCS Optumera | Retail merchandising AI | Wedge into retail clients' core decisions |
| TCS WisdomNext | GenAI orchestration | Platform for multi-model enterprise AI |

Strategic position matrix:

|  | Strong position | Building position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Large market | Core banking (BaNCS), modernisation services | Enterprise AI platforms (WisdomNext), AI infrastructure (HyperVault) |
| Niche market | Indian exam delivery (iON) | Retail AI (Optumera), AIOps (ignio) |

Wins and challenges:

- **Win:** BaNCS's recognition in Europe and continued large-bank deployments show the product can compete with Temenos and Finacle.
- **Win:** AI services crossing about US$2.3 billion annualised shows clients are buying AI work from TCS.
- **Challenge:** Revenue growth has been muted, and the company carried out a workforce restructuring of roughly 2% in 2025 (as reported), reflecting pressure on the traditional model.
- **Challenge:** Products remain less visible than rivals' brands among digital-first buyers.

## Short-Term: The Next 12 Months

Priorities likely to dominate TCS's product agenda:

1. **AI inside every product** BaNCS: AI copilots for bank operations, product configuration and compliance checks. ignio: GenAI-assisted runbook creation and natural-language operations. iON: AI-assisted evaluation and proctoring. MasterCraft: GenAI-assisted code understanding and conversion.
2. **Packaging AI for clients** Industry blueprints on WisdomNext for banking, insurance, retail and life sciences. Agent marketplaces and reusable agents for common enterprise tasks.
3. **HyperVault build-out** First data-centre capacity, anchored by AI partners, positioned for sovereign and enterprise AI workloads in India.
4. **Cloud-native product upgrades** Cloud-native BaNCS deployments with hyperscaler partners; SaaS-style packaging for mid-sized clients.

Expected challenges:

- Proving AI value with measurable business outcomes, not pilots.
- Pricing: shifting from effort-based to outcome-based contracts without hurting margins.
- Talent: retraining a very large workforce to work alongside AI.

> For PM candidates
> In the next year, almost every TCS product team will be asked "where does AI fit?" Interviewers value candidates who can identify the one or two AI features that create measurable client value, rather than listing many.

## Mid-Term: 1-5 Year Outlook

Market evolution:

- Enterprise AI moves from copilots to agents that complete multi-step workflows. Services firms that own agent platforms and domain data will capture more value.
- Core systems in banking, insurance and retail modernise onto the cloud, creating a large market for products like BaNCS and tools like TransformPlus.
- India becomes a major AI infrastructure location, driven by data residency rules and cost advantages.

Likely TCS moves:

1. **From services to "services-as-software"**: Packaging repeatable outcomes (for example, autonomous IT operations through ignio, or exam delivery through iON) as subscription or outcome-priced offerings.
2. **Platform consolidation**: Connecting WisdomNext, ignio and industry products into a common AI and data layer so clients buy an integrated stack.
3. **Vertical depth**: Deeper products in banking (BaNCS), retail (Optumera) and life sciences, where domain data is a moat.
4. **Infrastructure plus intelligence**: Offering clients compute (HyperVault), models (via partners) and applications (TCS products and services) together.

Competitive dynamics:

| Competitor | Likely move | Threat to TCS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accenture | Heavy AI investment, acquisitions, consulting-led | High in strategy-led AI deals |
| Infosys (Topaz, Finacle, Cobalt) | AI and banking product push | High in BFSI |
| Hyperscalers (AWS, Microsoft, Google) | Direct AI tooling and modernisation services | High in modernisation and AI platforms |
| AI labs | Direct enterprise sales of agents | Medium to high |
| HCLTech, Wipro, Cognizant | Similar AI and platform pivots | Medium |

## Long-Term: 5-10 Year Projection

Possible scenarios:

1. **Platform-led TCS (optimistic):** Products and platforms grow to 20–25% of revenue. BaNCS becomes a leading cloud-native core globally, ignio and successors run large shares of client operations autonomously, and HyperVault anchors India's enterprise AI infrastructure.
2. **AI-augmented services (base case):** TCS remains primarily a services company but with much higher revenue per employee, driven by AI. Products serve as differentiators rather than growth engines.
3. **Commoditisation (pessimistic):** AI labs and hyperscalers capture most of the value; services firms compete on price for integration work. Products help but do not offset the pressure.

Signals to watch:

- Share of TCS revenue from subscription, platform and outcome-based contracts.
- BaNCS new logo wins in cloud-native deals.
- HyperVault utilisation and anchor tenants.
- Revenue per employee trend.

## Strategic Recommendations

1. **Treat products as growth engines, not add-ons.** Give BaNCS, ignio and iON clearer P&L autonomy and product leadership, with investment tied to recurring revenue growth.
2. **Lead with lowest-risk modernisation.** Combine BaNCS, TransformPlus and cloud partners into a proposition for banks that fear big-bang core replacements.
3. **Make WisdomNext the AI control plane.** Position it as the governed, multi-model layer that sits above any model or hyperscaler, with strong cost and quality analytics.
4. **Productise outcomes.** Price ignio-led operations and iON exam delivery on outcomes (incident minutes avoided, exam reliability), building trust and recurring revenue.
5. **Build product talent.** Create a structured path from business analyst and engineer roles into product management, with domain and AI training.

Implementation priorities:

| Priority | Timeline | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI features in flagship products | 0–12 months | Product units |
| Cloud-native BaNCS and SaaS pricing | 0–24 months | BaNCS leadership |
| Outcome-priced offerings | 12–36 months | Service lines + products |
| HyperVault anchor clients | 12–36 months | HyperVault |

## Key Takeaways

- TCS is shifting from effort-based services to AI-led services, owned platforms and AI infrastructure.
- Products like BaNCS, ignio, iON and WisdomNext are central to differentiation, even if they are a small share of revenue.
- The biggest risk is AI shrinking billable effort faster than new revenue streams grow.
- For PM candidates, domain expertise plus AI product thinking is the most valuable combination at TCS.
- Expect interview questions on BaNCS competition, AI platform strategy, outcome-based pricing and modernisation trade-offs.
