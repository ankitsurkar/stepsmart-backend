## Executive Summary

In 2026, Bajaj Finserv is moving from a lending-led conglomerate of financial businesses towards an AI-first financial services platform for over 100 million Indian customers. Three strategic shifts define this moment:

1. AI at the core: Bajaj Finance's FINAI programme is reworking sales, service, risk, and operations with AI, and management has outlined a new consumer AI platform that would sit alongside the classic app.
2. Full ownership of insurance: After buying out Allianz's stake, the group now fully controls Bajaj General Insurance and Bajaj Life Insurance, which opens the door to deeper integration with the lending franchise.
3. Payments and engagement: Bajaj Pay (UPI, wallet, bill payments) is the group's attempt to turn a monthly EMI relationship into a daily one.

Bajaj Finance reported a customer franchise of about 119 million by March 2026 and assets under management above ₹4.6 lakh crore by September 2025. The challenge is no longer reach. It is engagement, cost to serve, and staying relevant as UPI-based credit from payment apps and banks competes for the same customers.

The strategic direction is clear: use data from the largest consumer credit franchise in India, plus AI, to serve every financial need of a household, from a first phone on EMI to a home loan, term plan, and mutual fund.

## Introduction

Bajaj Finance's public commitment to AI, including hundreds of autonomous agents across functions and a new consumer AI experience planned around 2027, marks a turning point. It mirrors a wider trend in Indian financial services, where large lenders and payment apps are racing to cut costs and personalise at scale.

The landscape is also shifting: UPI has become the default way to pay, RBI has allowed credit lines on UPI, regulators have tightened digital lending norms, and new entrants with telecom or payments reach are moving into credit. In this context, Bajaj Finserv faces several strategic questions:

1. How does it keep its point-of-sale lending edge as payments move to UPI?
2. How far should AI replace human sales and service, and at what risk?
3. How should the group integrate lending, insurance, health, and the Bajaj Markets marketplace without conflicts?

This guide looks at the current product landscape, short-term priorities, mid-term outlook, and long-term vision.

## Bajaj Finserv's Current Product Landscape

Bajaj Finserv's businesses span lending, insurance, payments, health, and distribution. The company does not publish a product-level revenue split, but the broad picture is:

- Lending (Bajaj Finance and Bajaj Housing Finance): the largest contributor, covering consumer durable loans, Insta EMI Card, personal loans, two- and three-wheeler loans, gold loans, SME and commercial loans, and home loans.
- Insurance (Bajaj General Insurance, Bajaj Life Insurance): large premium franchises sold through agents, banks, and the group's own lending customers.
- Payments (Bajaj Pay): UPI, wallet, UPI Lite, bill payments, and recharges inside the Bajaj Finserv app.
- Health (Bajaj Finserv Health): Aarogya Care prepaid plans, OPD cover, teleconsults, and provider network.
- Marketplace (Bajaj Markets): third-party loans, cards, insurance, and investments.
- Asset management and broking: newer businesses building scale.

Market position:

| Strong Position | Building Position |
| --- | --- |
| Consumer durable financing at the point of sale | UPI payments (Bajaj Pay) |
| Pre-approved credit to an existing franchise | Health-tech (Bajaj Finserv Health) |
| Insurance distribution to borrowers | Open marketplace (Bajaj Markets) |
| Offline reach in smaller towns | Asset management |

Recent wins and challenges:

- Win: Growth of the customer franchise to about 119 million, with customers using several products on average.
- Win: AI bots handling a large majority of service resolutions in FY26, according to company disclosures.
- Challenge: Low daily engagement compared with payment apps; most customers open the app for a task and leave.
- Challenge: Periods of regulatory scrutiny of digital lending across the sector, which shape how fast new journeys can launch.

## Short-Term: The Next 12 Months

Three themes dominate:

1. AI-led service and operations Scale conversational AI across products and languages Expand voice bots for collections and service calls Success metric: lower cost to serve per customer with stable or better complaint ratios
2. Credit on UPI and Bajaj Pay engagement Link EMI limits and credit lines to UPI payments Extend partner-store offers to UPI QR payments Success metric: monthly active Bajaj Pay payers among loan customers
3. Insurance integration after the Allianz exit Unify brand and journeys for Bajaj General and Bajaj Life inside the Bajaj Finserv app Bundle protection with loans in a way that is transparent and compliant Success metric: insurance attach rate on loans without higher complaint rates

Risks in this period include regulatory changes on credit-on-UPI, AI errors at scale, and festive-season execution, when consumer durable volumes peak.

## Mid-Term: 1-5 Year Outlook

Market evolution:

- UPI-based credit becomes common; cards and EMI products compete on the same rails.
- Account Aggregator and the Unified Lending Interface make cash-flow-based underwriting mainstream.
- AI assistants handle a large share of financial tasks for mass-market customers.

Strategic moves:

1. Consumer AI platform: A goal-based "AI mode" where customers describe what they want and the system builds a plan across loans, insurance, and savings.
2. Household financial graph: Combining lending, insurance, health, and payments data (with consent) to serve the household, not just the borrower.
3. Rural and semi-urban depth: Using voice and vernacular AI to serve customers whom field teams reach today.
4. Open distribution: Offering Bajaj credit inside third-party apps and ecosystems while keeping the best experience in Bajaj's own app.

| Initiative | Expected Benefit | Key Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consumer AI platform | Higher products per customer, lower service cost | Mis-selling, regulatory scrutiny |
| Household graph | Better underwriting and cross-sell | Privacy and consent complexity |
| Vernacular AI in rural markets | New customer acquisition | Credit risk in thin-file segments |
| Embedded credit | Reach beyond own app | Margin pressure, partner dependency |

## Long-Term: 5-10 Year Projection

Industry transformation:

- Financial services in India become largely invisible, embedded in commerce, payments, and AI assistants.
- Competition shifts from product features to data, trust, and cost of capital.
- Regulators set clearer rules for AI-led advice and lending.

Bajaj Finserv's potential evolution:

- From lender to full-stack household finance platform: credit, protection, health, and wealth managed together.
- From app-first to assistant-first: much of the interaction happens through AI agents, voice, and messaging.
- From domestic leader to a reference model for emerging-market consumer finance.

Potential disruptors:

- Big tech and telecom-backed finance players with daily engagement
- Banks that close the digital gap and use low-cost deposits to undercut lending rates
- Regulation limiting how AI can recommend credit

## Strategic Recommendations

1. Make AI trustworthy before making it ambitious: Scale AI in service first, build strong evaluation and governance, then move to AI-led credit recommendations.
2. Own the moment of payment: Use Bajaj Pay and credit on UPI to stay present as payments leave card rails.
3. Integrate insurance with discipline: Use full ownership of the insurance companies to build simple, honest bundles, avoiding forced cross-sell.
4. Serve the household: Build consented, household-level views to deepen relationships across lending, health, and protection.

Success metrics to watch:

- Products per customer
- Cost to serve per customer
- Monthly active app and Bajaj Pay users
- Delinquency and complaint ratios on AI-originated business

Key risks and mitigation:

- Regulatory risk: early engagement with RBI and IRDAI; explainable AI decisions.
- AI failure at scale: staged rollouts, kill switches, human oversight.
- Competitive erosion at point of sale: credit on UPI and exclusive merchant offers.

Timeline of expected shifts:

- Year 1: AI service at scale, credit on UPI pilots, unified insurance journeys
- Years 2–3: Consumer AI platform live, household graph, vernacular AI in rural markets
- Years 3–5: Embedded distribution at scale, assistant-first engagement

## Key Takeaways

Bajaj Finserv's future depends on three moves:

1. Turning AI from a cost-cutting tool into a better customer experience without compromising credit discipline.
2. Staying present at the moment of payment as UPI reshapes how Indians buy and borrow.
3. Combining lending, insurance, and health into a coherent household offer now that the group fully owns its insurers.

For PM candidates, these themes are the backdrop for most case questions. Expect interviewers to probe how you would balance growth, AI, and risk.

Bottom line: Bajaj Finserv has unmatched reach and data in Indian consumer finance. The next few years will show whether it can turn that reach into daily relevance and AI-powered service while keeping the risk discipline that built the franchise.
