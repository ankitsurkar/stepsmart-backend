## Executive Summary

In 2026 Cohere is at a turning point. Having started as a model API company, it now sells a full enterprise AI stack: Command models for generation and reasoning, Embed and Rerank for retrieval, Compass for enterprise search, North as an agentic workspace, and Model Vault for private deployment. Three strategic shifts define its direction:

1. From models to platform: North and its agents are becoming the main product customers buy, with models underneath.
2. From North America to transatlantic: the planned combination with Aleph Alpha, backed by Germany's Schwarz Group, gives Cohere a European base and public-sector relationships.
3. From closed to selectively open: Command A+, released in 2026 under the Apache 2.0 licence, uses openness to win trust with governments and developers.

Reports suggest Cohere ended 2025 with roughly USD 240 million in annualised revenue and that most revenue comes from private deployments. Its strategy is to be the default AI provider for organisations that cannot or will not send sensitive data to consumer-focused AI labs.

## Introduction

Cohere's competitive position is unusual. It competes with OpenAI, Anthropic and Google on model quality, with Microsoft and Google on workplace assistants, and with Mistral AI and open-source models on control and cost. It cannot outspend these rivals, so it competes on focus: enterprise and government only, private deployment first, and strong retrieval.

Key strategic questions:

1. Can Cohere's platform (North) beat bundled assistants such as Microsoft 365 Copilot inside large enterprises?
2. How should it integrate Aleph Alpha and turn sovereignty into durable revenue?
3. How can it keep model quality competitive with much better-funded labs?

This guide covers Cohere's current product landscape, its likely short, mid and long-term moves, and recommendations PM candidates can use in strategy interviews.

## Cohere's Current Product Landscape

Estimated revenue mix (reported or inferred from public sources, not company-disclosed):

- Private deployments of models and North: about 80-85%
- Public API usage (Command, Embed, Rerank): about 10-15%
- Cloud marketplace and partner channels: the remainder

Core products:

| Product | What it does | Strategic role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Command A family (including Reasoning, Vision, A+) | Generation, reasoning, tool use, multilingual | Core intelligence; efficiency on few GPUs |
| Embed | Text and image embeddings for search | Retrieval foundation; developer entry point |
| Rerank | Re-orders search results by relevance | Widely adopted wedge into enterprise search |
| Compass | Enterprise search across documents and data | Powers grounded answers in North |
| North | Agentic AI workspace with chat, search and agents | Main enterprise application, drives seat revenue |
| Model Vault | Isolated deployment platform in VPC or on-premises | Makes private deployment repeatable |

> Strategic Insight
> Cohere's biggest asset is not one model but the combination of efficient models and private deployment. Few rivals can put a complete, supported AI stack inside a bank's own data centre.

Key partnerships:

- Bell Canada: sovereign AI offering bringing North to Canadian organisations (2025).
- Cloud providers: Oracle, AWS, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud marketplaces.
- Enterprise and integration partners such as SAP, Fujitsu and LG CNS (reported).

## Short-Term: The Next 12 Months

Priority 1: Complete and integrate the Aleph Alpha combination

- Guarantee continuity for existing Aleph Alpha customers in Germany.
- Build a single roadmap with North as the core platform.
- Use Schwarz Group's sovereign cloud (STACKIT) as a reference deployment for European customers.

Priority 2: Make North the standard for regulated enterprises

- Role-based agent templates for banking, insurance, telecom and public sector.
- More connectors and better permission-aware retrieval.
- Admin dashboards that show sponsors measurable productivity gains.

Priority 3: Grow Managed North

- A Cohere-operated, isolated version for mid-size companies that want privacy without running infrastructure.
- Faster time to production (days rather than months).

Priority 4: Monetise the open model strategy

- Distribute Command A+ widely.
- Sell a production package: support, security patches, indemnity and one-click deployment through Model Vault.

| Initiative | Expected impact | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aleph Alpha integration | European public-sector revenue | Customer churn during migration |
| North agent templates | Faster adoption and seat expansion | Template quality varies by industry |
| Managed North | More mid-size customers | GPU costs and margins |
| Command A+ production pack | New pipeline from open-model users | Cannibalisation of paid model access |

## Mid-Term: 1-5 Year Outlook

1. Sovereign AI as a category: European and Canadian governments are likely to keep funding domestic or allied AI capability. Cohere can become the default provider for countries that want an alternative to US hyperscalers, especially in defence, healthcare and public administration.
2. Agents as the main product: as agents handle multi-step work (preparing reports, processing claims, answering procurement questions), pricing may move from seats to outcomes or work completed. Cohere will need strong agent evaluation, auditing and human-approval tools, because regulated customers must explain every automated decision.
3. Model efficiency as a moat: Cohere's models are designed to run on few GPUs. As customers deploy privately and pay for their own hardware, efficient models lower total cost and become a key selling point.
4. Possible public listing: reports and leadership comments suggest Cohere is preparing for an eventual IPO. That will raise pressure for predictable revenue, healthy gross margins and clear segment reporting.

> Key Risk
> Bundling is the biggest threat. If Microsoft, Google or Salesforce offer "good enough" AI inside tools customers already pay for, Cohere must prove clearly superior security, accuracy or cost to win.

## Long-Term: 5-10 Year Projection

Scenario 1: Trusted AI infrastructure provider (most likely if execution holds) Cohere becomes to enterprise AI what established database vendors became to enterprise data: a trusted, deployable platform that sits inside critical systems for decades.

Scenario 2: Sovereign champion Cohere becomes primarily a strategic supplier to allied governments and critical industries, with a smaller but highly defensible business.

Scenario 3: Absorbed by a larger player If model costs keep rising and bundling wins, Cohere could become an acquisition target for a cloud provider, enterprise software company or telecom group.

Long-term product bets:

- Autonomous back-office agents for regulated processes with full audit trails.
- Industry-specific models trained with customers' consent on domain data (finance, legal, healthcare).
- Cross-organisation agents that work securely between companies and governments.

## Strategic Recommendations

1. Lead with retrieval quality and governance. Answers in regulated industries must be correct and traceable. Investing in Compass, Embed and Rerank keeps North differentiated even if rival models are stronger at general tasks.
2. Treat continuity as the integration's first product. Aleph Alpha's customers chose a German provider for trust; losing them would undermine the sovereignty story.
3. Productise deployment. Security evidence packs, capacity planning and one-click upgrades shorten the slowest part of every deal.
4. Price for value, not tokens. Seat and outcome-based pricing for North aligns Cohere with the productivity gains sponsors care about.
5. Use openness carefully. Keep releasing efficient open models to build trust, but keep the platform, enterprise-tuned retrieval and support paid.

| Recommendation | Timeline | Owner area |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retrieval and governance investment | Ongoing | Search and retrieval PMs |
| Continuity programme | First 6 months post-close | Customer success, North PMs |
| Deployment productisation | 6-12 months | Platform PMs |
| Value-based pricing pilots | 6-18 months | Commercial and North PMs |
| Open-core policy | 3 months | Model product PMs |

## Key Takeaways

1. Cohere's strategy is focus: enterprise and government only, private deployment first.
2. North is becoming the main product, with models and retrieval as its engine.
3. The Aleph Alpha combination turns sovereignty into a transatlantic growth strategy.
4. Efficiency and open weights are tools for trust and cost, not ends in themselves.
5. The main risks are bundled competitors, model cost escalation and integration execution.

For PM candidates: in strategy interviews, show you understand that Cohere wins by being the safest, most controllable option, and connect product ideas to that position.
