## Executive Summary

In 2026, Cleartrip is in transition from a discount-led flight booking brand to a multi-category travel platform that has to pay its own way. Three shifts define its strategy:

1. **Beyond flights:** Flights bring about 80% of revenue. Cleartrip is pushing hotels, trains (launched through IRCTC in April 2026), buses and packages to change that mix.
2. **From discounts to efficiency:** Reported FY26 figures show a net loss of about ₹805 crore and discounts and cashbacks of roughly ₹900 crore. The company has targeted operational breakeven by the end of FY27.
3. **Ecosystem advantage:** Flipkart's reach, payments and SuperCoins are tools no other Indian online travel agency (OTA) has.

Company leaders describe Cleartrip as India's second-largest air-bookings player and a "fourth-ish" hotel player. The strategic question is whether it can keep its flight position while building hotels and ancillaries into a profitable engine.

## Introduction

Cleartrip's 2026 launches show the direction: Price Trends for flight fares, Live Flight Status for disruption-hit travellers, Explore Hotels by Vibe for mood-based hotel discovery, Book@0 for card-free hotel holds, and train booking with IRCTC. Each either grows a non-flight category or makes the flight experience stickier without more cashback.

The Indian online travel market is growing on the back of rising air traffic, new regional airports and stronger leisure demand. But it is crowded. MakeMyTrip (with Goibibo) leads and is profitable, ixigo has built a profitable trains-buses-flights platform with AI features, EaseMyTrip competes hard on price, and airlines push direct booking. Cleartrip faces three questions:

1. How can it grow hotels fast enough to reduce its reliance on flights?
2. How can it cut discount spend without losing its number-two air position?
3. How can it turn Flipkart's ecosystem into a lasting advantage?

## Cleartrip's Current Product Landscape

Estimated revenue mix (based on public statements and industry estimates):

- Flights (domestic and international): about 75–80%
- Hotels: about 12–15%
- Ancillaries, trains, buses, packages and other: about 5–10%

Key products:

- **Flights:** Core booking with CT Flexmax (free cancellation and rescheduling up to 24 hours before departure), Price Trends and Live Flight Status.
- **Hotels:** Domestic and international inventory, Explore Hotels by Vibe, Book@0 pay-later holds.
- **Trains and buses:** IRCTC-powered trains (2026) and bus booking.
- **Cleartrip on Flipkart:** The travel tab inside the Flipkart app, SuperCoins earn and burn, Flipkart payment options.
- **Cleartrip for Business:** Self-serve business travel for companies.
- **Middle East:** Cleartrip operates in the Gulf region and acquired Saudi-based Flyin in 2022.

Recent wins and losses:

- **Win:** Strong growth in air share, helped by Flipkart distribution and sale events.
- **Loss:** Losses widened in FY26 as discounts grew faster than revenue.

Strategic Position Matrix:

| High Market Share | Low Market Share |
| --- | --- |
| Domestic flights (top two) | Hotels (around fourth) |
| Flipkart-sourced travellers | Trains (new entrant against IRCTC and ixigo) |

## Short-Term: The Next 12 Months

Cleartrip's short-term priorities appear to follow three themes:

1. **Discount efficiency** Replace blanket cashback with targeted offers and SuperCoins Measure incrementality with holdout groups Success metric: discount spend as a share of gross bookings falls meaningfully while air share stays within a set floor
2. **Hotel acceleration** Cross-sell hotels in the flight journey for leisure trips Scale Book@0 and Explore by Vibe to more destinations Success metric: hotel share of revenue rises toward 20%
3. **New categories for frequency** Improve train booking conversion (IRCTC login, waitlist predictions) Use trains and buses to bring users back between flights Success metric: trains reach the company's stated goal of 5–10% of business contribution

Strategic Dialogue Section: Three questions come up repeatedly when analysts discuss Cleartrip's near-term plan:

1. Can Cleartrip cut discounts without losing share to MakeMyTrip and EaseMyTrip? The likely answer is targeting: protect high-value travellers, stop subsidising deal hunters who never return.
2. Is trains a profitable business? Probably not directly, given thin margins and IRCTC's dominance. Its value is frequency and cross-sell.
3. How much can Flipkart help? Flipkart's reach is clear, but homepage space and SuperCoins budgets are shared with every Flipkart category, so Cleartrip must prove travel's value to the wider ecosystem.

## Mid-Term: 1-5 Year Outlook

Cleartrip's mid-term strategy is likely to rest on three bets:

1. **A real hotel business:** Deeper supply in leisure destinations, partner-funded deals, better ranking and content. Hotels should become the main profit engine.
2. **Ancillaries and flexibility:** Extend the Flexmax idea across categories (hotels, trains, international trips), add travel insurance and visa services, and price by risk.
3. **Loyalty across Flipkart and Cleartrip:** A single loyalty tier that rewards both shopping and travel, with travel perks like free flexibility or lounge access.

Market evolution to watch:

- Airlines will keep pushing direct sales and new distribution standards (NDC), squeezing OTA flight margins.
- AI trip planning will raise expectations for personalised, conversational booking; ixigo has already relaunched its app around an AI assistant.
- Regulation on dark patterns and transparent pricing will shape how ancillaries and fees are sold.

| Bet | Expected Impact | Main Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hotel business | Better margins, less flight dependence | MakeMyTrip's supply depth |
| Ancillaries | Higher revenue per trip | Regulatory scrutiny, trust |
| Joint loyalty | Higher frequency and retention | Cost and Flipkart alignment |

## Long-Term: 5-10 Year Projection

Looking further ahead, Cleartrip's position depends on how three forces play out:

1. **AI-native travel planning:** Booking may shift from search-and-filter to conversational planning. OTAs with good data, inventory and service operations will power these experiences, whether in their own apps or through AI assistants.
2. **Commerce and travel convergence:** As e-commerce platforms build financial services and loyalty, travel may become one part of a broader consumer membership. Cleartrip's ownership by Flipkart positions it well for this.
3. **International outbound travel:** Rising Indian incomes will drive more international trips, where order values and margins are higher. Cleartrip's Middle East presence could support this.

Potential long-term moves:

- Become the travel layer of a Flipkart membership programme.
- Offer travel services to other platforms (B2B2C), using Cleartrip's booking engine.
- Use AI agents for trip servicing (rebooking, refunds), which reduces support costs.

## Strategic Recommendations

For Cleartrip's leadership:

1. **Make contribution margin per trip the company's core metric.** It aligns flight, hotel and offers teams around profitable growth.
2. **Invest in hotels first, trains second.** Hotels change the economics; trains build habit.
3. **Turn discounts into loyalty.** Shift spend from one-off cashback to SuperCoins and perks that bring users back.

For product managers at Cleartrip:

1. Always quantify the margin effect of a feature, not just conversion.
2. Design for the full trip, from planning to return, because cross-sell lives in the gaps.
3. Treat Flipkart as a partner with its own goals, and frame proposals in terms of shared value.

For candidates preparing for Cleartrip interviews:

1. Know the business model: commissions, convenience fees, ancillaries and hotel margins.
2. Study recent launches (Book@0, Price Trends, train booking) and form a view on each.
3. Be ready to discuss trade-offs between growth and profitability.

| Recommendation | Time Horizon | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discount incrementality programme | 0–6 months | Offers and growth |
| Hotel cross-sell in flight journey | 0–12 months | Hotels and air product |
| Cross-category flexibility products | 6–24 months | Ancillaries |
| Joint loyalty with Flipkart | 12–36 months | Leadership and platform |

## Key Takeaways

1. Cleartrip is strong in flights but needs hotels and ancillaries to reach breakeven.
2. Discount efficiency is the fastest profit lever, but must be targeted to protect share.
3. Flipkart's ecosystem is Cleartrip's biggest advantage and its biggest dependency.
4. Trains and buses are frequency tools rather than profit centres.
5. AI-led planning and servicing will shape the next phase of competition.

Bottom Line: Cleartrip's path forward is not about winning more flight bookings at any cost. It is about making every trip profitable, using flights as the front door and hotels, flexibility and loyalty as the business.
